There are so many things to do in Singapore; I’ve experienced most of them. And so, after my most recent visit, I decided it was high time I put this guide together.

I took my first steps in this country *way* back in 2011, when I was just four months into this… well, rather extended trip around the world.

And my first impressions? I’ll confess I thought Singapore was boring.

It was clean and polished and *[expensive](https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/cost-of-travel-singapore-budget/)*, with numerous rules and regulations. It felt safe and organised, and almost everybody spoke English. Roaming its streets felt like travel on easy mode but at that time in my life, I was fulfilled by more challenging destinations.

It sounds like a ridiculous reason not to vibe with a place, but hey, I was in my twenties and travelling for the very first time; I was kind of an idiot. At that stage in my trip, I craved chaos, confusion, and misadventure at every turn. I wanted my culture shock to be cranked all the way up to 11.

Needless to say, I no longer feel this way!

These days, I delight in every return trip I get to make to Singapore. I regularly rock up in this city on yet another three-day layover with dozens of new attractions on my list to check out. I love travelling in a country where everything just *works*. I don’t have to worry about getting scammed or lost or sick or taken advantage of. I can walk around at night on my own and not feel concerned for my safety.

Visiting Singapore feels like a breath of fresh air and I can’t get enough of it; this year, I’ve even visited twice already. At this stage in my Singapore travel career, I feel like I’ve experienced the vast majority of attractions in the country.

Today, I’m going to be sharing all of the best things to do in the city.

## Hit Up the Impressive Gardens by the Bay

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Inside-Gardens-by-the-Bay-Cloud-Forest-Singapore.jpg)

I was so impressed with the Cloud Forest at Gardens by the Bay!

There’s nowhere on earth quite like Gardens by the Bay.

If you were to come to Singapore and do only one thing, I’d insist you make it Gardens by the Bay. This huge, futuristic park is *such* an impressive complex, especially if you love immersing yourself in nature. You’ve got soaring Supertrees, giant greenhouses, elevated walkways, and flower-filled domes; all of them packed full of plants from around the world.

Most people find they need a minimum of three hours to fully explore it all but I’ve often found myself spending four hours wandering around. Here’s what you need to know before you go:

- Despite the fact that my photo was taken at sunset, I actually recommend heading to Gardens by the Bay when it opens to beat the crowds. I arrived at the Cloud Forest at 8:55 a.m. on my most recent trip, five minutes before opening time. Amazingly, I was the very first person to step foot inside the dome! What an incredible experience. Visiting in the morning also means that if you *really* find yourself delighting in the greenery, you won’t have to worry about the impending closure of the domes; speaking from experience here, as I ran out of time during my first visit.
- The entrance to Gardens by the Bay is a solid 15-minute walk from both of the nearest MRT stations (Gardens by the Bay and Bayfront); Bayfront station runs a S$3 round-trip shuttle to the entrance for those of you who don’t want to walk in the heat. Plan accordingly if you’re aiming to get there at opening time. I arrived at the Gardens by the Bay MTR station at 8:35 a.m. to give myself plenty of time.
- The ticketing situation is inexplicably complex. For some reason, Gardens by the Bay does *not* offer a single all-in-one ticket that gives you access to every attraction in the park. They don’t even offer the option to buy access to one single conservatory. Instead, they offer a series of combo tickets, with each including two or three different parts of the park. Even more strangely, there’s a wider variety of ticket combinations [on Get Your Guide](https://www.getyourguide.com/singapore-l170/singapore-gardens-by-the-bay-day-admission-tickets-t117975/?partner_id=5OA45ES&utm_medium=online_publisher&cmp=singapore-activities&mkt_cmp=true&deeplink_id=b63abc6a-c190-59a4-bef8-3fc8b7f90b41&page_id=ccae3146-853d-5676-b54a-aa296c4ed2af) than there is on [the official park’s website](https://ticket2.gardensbythebay.com.sg/)! All that to say, figuring out what to buy was a massive pain in the ass, so here’s what I recommend:
	- If you want to see it all, go for the [Flower Dome, Cloud Forest, and Floral Fantasy ticket](https://www.getyourguide.com/singapore-l170/singapore-gardens-by-the-bay-day-admission-tickets-t117975/?partner_id=5OA45ES&utm_medium=online_publisher&cmp=singapore-activities&mkt_cmp=true&deeplink_id=b63abc6a-c190-59a4-bef8-3fc8b7f90b41&page_id=ccae3146-853d-5676-b54a-aa296c4ed2af), which gives you access to all three of the conservatories, then buy the [OCBC Skyway ticket](https://ticket2.gardensbythebay.com.sg/events/ocbc-skyway/GB22SWPD), which takes you up on the elevated walkway
		- If you just want to see the best parts of the complex, skip the Floral Fantasy area (I’ve never been very impressed by it) and instead, opt for [the Flower Dome and Cloud Forest ticket](https://www.getyourguide.com/singapore-l170/singapore-gardens-by-the-bay-day-admission-tickets-t117975/?partner_id=5OA45ES&utm_medium=online_publisher&cmp=singapore-activities&mkt_cmp=true&deeplink_id=b63abc6a-c190-59a4-bef8-3fc8b7f90b41&page_id=ccae3146-853d-5676-b54a-aa296c4ed2af). Again, pair it with the [OCBC Skyway ticket](https://ticket2.gardensbythebay.com.sg/events/ocbc-skyway/GB22SWPD).
		- If you’re on a tight budget, get the [Cloud Forest and Supertree Observatory ticket](https://ticket2.gardensbythebay.com.sg/events/cloud-forest-%2B-supertree-observatory/CFSO2024), as that’ll give you a good overview of the most impressive parts of the park.

*See what I mean about it all being unnecessarily complicated?*

Still, I promise you that it’s worth battling through the ticket-purchasing rigmarole, as you’ll get to spend half a day feeling as though you’ve wandered on to the set of a sci-fi movie.

## Watch Garden Rhapsody: the Free Light Show at Gardens by the Bay

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Singapore-Gardens-By-the-Bay-night-show.jpg)

Garden Rhapsody from the ground…

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Garden-Rhapsody-Singapore-Walkway.jpg)

… and from the Skyway!

Yes, there’s still more to do at Gardens by the Bay!

Every single night, the park runs Garden Rhapsody, a light-and-music show that’s completely free to visit. There’s no reason not to check it out while you’re in town! You’ve also got two different options for watching this magical show; both pictured above.

I would say that if it’s possible for you to experience the Garden Rhapsody from the Skyway, you should absolutely make the effort to. I thought that strolling along that walkway while music and lights pulsed around me was one of the most magical moments of my life; at times, I had a lump in my throat because it felt so incredible.

The problem is: *everybody* wants to experience the light show from the Skyway! I really lucked out here, as I managed to time my Skyway walk perfectly; I stepped outside of the elevator right as the first note sounded out across the complex!

## Dedicate an Afternoon to the Sizeable Botanic Gardens

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Singapore-Botanic-Gardens-lake.jpg)

I’ve been to botanic gardens in 50-odd countries and I’d say that Singapore’s is the world’s best!

Singapore is paradise if you love yourself some greenery.

The city’s botanic gardens are some of the best I’ve ever experienced, and I couldn’t believe just how extensive the grounds were. The gardens are so impressive that they’re the only one in the world that’s a UNESCO World Heritage Site! I’d recommend allocating a minimum of two hours to exploring the entire complex, but to not be surprised if you end up lingering for three or four.

Unlike many of Singapore’s attractions, entry to the Botanic Gardens is completely free. It’s also open between 5 a.m. and midnight, which makes it an excellent spot for taking a morning/evening run when the city is at its least humid.

One of the main highlights of the Botanic Gardens is the National Orchid Garden, which does have an entrance fee of S$15. *Is it worth it?* Absolutely! Even if you don’t consider yourself a flower person. The garden is home to more than 1,500 species of orchids (and over 3,000 hybrids!) and it’s impressive to see so much colour and variety in one place. I found it fun to wander the VIP Orchid Garden, where the hybrids have been names after visiting celebrities

Outside of the Orchid Garden, make sure you prioritise checking out the Ginger Garden (who knew there are over 100 species of ginger?), the beautiful Swan Lake, the Rainforest Trail, the Healing Garden (home to hundreds of medicinal plants), the Heliconia Walk (full of unusual-looking flowers), the Bandstand (great for taking photos), and the Prisoner of War Steps (built by Australian Prisoners of War in the 1940s during the Japanese Occupation).

## Have Sunset Cocktails at the Marina Bay Sands

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/View-from-Marina-Bay-Sands-at-night.jpg)

One of my Singapore highlights was drinking Singapore slings at Marina Bay Sands at sunset!

There’s no doubt about it: the most iconic structure in Singapore *has* to be the Marina Bay Sands. This trio of towering buildings is connected at the top by a ship (well, at least it *looks* like a ship), which in turn is home to most incredible infinity pool overlooking the city.

The infinity pool isn’t for everyone. In fact, only guests of the hotel can access the pool — and it’s one of the priciest properties in Singapore. [Stays at the hotel start from $500 a night](https://www.booking.com/hotel/sg/marina-bay-sands.en.html?aid=1514105&no_rooms=1&group_adults=2&label=singapore-activities), so this is definitely one for a special occasion.

If your budget can’t quite stretch that far, you’ll still have the opportunity to experience a small taste of the hotel’s luxury, because everyone the general public can access [the SkyPark Observation Deck](https://www.getyourguide.com/marina-bay-sands-skypark-l87298/marina-bay-sands-skypark-observation-deck-ticket-t131787/?partner_id=5OA45ES&utm_medium=online_publisher&cmp=singapore-activities&mkt_cmp=true&deeplink_id=d4bb4a61-cf8b-5e72-9556-38f0006c6da3&page_id=ccae3146-853d-5676-b54a-aa296c4ed2af) (S$35)at the Marina Bay Sands. Located on the 57th floor, the views from up here are unrivalled. In my opinion, the best time to visit is at sunset — you can see the epic views in my photo above, but here’s the view from another angle, too:

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Marina-Bay-Sands-night-view.jpg)

Yes, you can even watch the Gardens by the Bay light show from the Marina Bay Sands!

Grab yourself a cold Singapore sling and wait for the sunset; in either direction, the views are spectacular.

You’ll either be looking towards the dense city, packed full of skyscrapers, or out towards the water, with greenery, gardens, and dozens of ships. To me, that summarises Singapore perfectly: skyscrapers, ships, and scenery! And yes, one of the best parts of being up in the SkyPark is getting to watch the Gardens by the Bay light show from above.

I saved visiting Marina Bay Sands for my final night in Singapore and it was the perfect send-off for my time in the city. I got to toast to a wonderful trip, watch the city begin to light up, and point out all the spots that had made my trip so special.

## Go Mural-Spotting Through Chinatown

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Mural-Tiong-Bahru-Singapore.jpg)

This was one of my favourite murals in Chinatown!

One of my favourite areas to explore in Singapore is Chinatown, and that’s mostly due to the incredibly photogenic murals you find all over the neighbourhood.

And they really are everywhere; most of the streets I wandered down had beautiful murals painted on the walls, and they were all in incredible condition. I love the nostalgic vibe of the artwork, too, as they focus on everyday life in Singapore from decades gone by: letter writers hunched over wooden desks, traditional barbers at work, and family life in cramped shophouses.

Many of the most famous pieces were created by Singaporean artist Yip Yew Chong, whose work has transformed otherwise ordinary alleyways into open-air museums.

Expect to spend an hour wandering through the streets and hunting down as many as possible.

## Marvel at the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Buddha-Tooth-Relic-Temple-Singapore.jpg)

I’ve never seen a temple like the Buddha Tooth Relic before!

Speaking of Chinatown, you can’t visit this bustling neighbourhood and *not* pay a visit to the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple.

Despite being a relatively modern building in Singapore (it was built in 2007), it’s still well-worth checking out, and makes for a relaxing break if you’re feeling overwhelmed by the chaos of Chinatown.

The temple is said to house the canine tooth of the Buddha (the clue’s in the name!) and it’s displayed in an elaborate gold stupa on the top floor. I spent half an hour wandering through the different rooms and levels, and would recommend taking a look inside, even if you’re not religious, as it’s totally free to explore. There’s even a rooftop garden on the top floor offering up views over the area.

Keep in mind that guests are expected to cover their shoulders and knees when inside the temple, but they do have wraps you can use if you forget.

## Learn More at the National Museum of Singapore

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Inside-Singapore-National-Museum.jpg)

I spent a fun morning learning all about Singapore’s history at its National Museum

**Note:** Parts of Singapore’s National Museum are undergoing restoration work right now, including the Singapore History Gallery (which reopens in October 2026). Because of this, I wouldn’t recommend visiting the museum unless you’re there post-the-October-reopening, as you’ll be missing out on all of the best parts!

I’ll be honest: I’m really not that into museums. I tend to speed-sightsee my way around them, glancing at plaques and attempting to *get the gist* in record time.

You shouldn’t be surprised, then, when I confess that it took half a dozen trips to Singapore for me to finally step inside the city’s [National Museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.nhb.gov.sg/plan-your-visit/visitor-information/admissions). You might be surprised, however, when I tell you that I loved my time there.

I didn’t know a huge amount about Singapore’s fascinating history before I finally made my way to the museum and I realised what an oversight that had been when I finally took the time to learn more.

What I loved most about the museum — and what I strongly believe all museums should do! — is that it focused more on storytelling and personal accounts, which helped put the country’s history into proper context.

Instead of dry, lengthy descriptions of artefacts that few people care about, you instead get to learn what it was like to live in Singapore throughout history, from early settlements to British colonial rule to the Japanese Occupation to its independence and subsequent rapid development.

If you visit just one museum in Singapore, I’d wholeheartedly recommend this one.

## Stroll Around Fort Canning Park

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Fort-Canning-Tree-Tunnel-edited.jpg)

The Fort Canning Tree Tunnel provides one of the most popular views in all of Singapore! Go early or you’ll be queueing behind 30 influencers to take your shot

Singapore’s National Museum is located within the grounds of Fort Canning Park, so you’ll likely spend an hour or two exploring this area before or after your visit. (I recommend after the museum, so that you can put the history you just learned about into perspective).

The fort sits atop a small hill that has been important in Singapore for centuries.

Long before the skyscrapers arrived, this was the seat of Malay royalty. Later, the British turned it into a military stronghold, building barracks, bunkers, and command centres here. During World War II, it became a key British headquarters before Singapore’s surrender. Today, all of that history is still visible throughout the park, but instead of feeling heavy, it’s part of a peaceful landscape of trees, lawns, and winding paths.

One of the most popular spots in the park is depicted in my photo above: the Fort Canning Tree Tunnel. It’s such a beautiful part of the complex, but as I say in my caption: the vibes are absolutely ruined by the plethora of influencers who head straight here for the posing opportunities. To get my photo, I waited for one girl to finish and head out of the shot, then snapped my photo before the next one made her way up the steps.

Outside of Influencer Central, there’s plenty of military-themed areas to explore in Fort Canning. There’s the Battlebox (an underground British commander bunker you can tour for free) and the Fort Canning Centre (former British barracks that have been transformed in a free gallery, showcasing artefacts discovered in the park).

There’s various different gardens on the grounds, too (this is Singapore, after all!), like the Spice Garden, Raffles Garden, the Sang Nila Utama Garden, and (my favourite!) the Forbidden Spring.

If this sounds like your kind of place, I can highly recommend [the 2.5 hour walking tour I took around Fort Canning](https://www.getyourguide.com/singapore-l170/singapore-walking-tour-of-graves-guns-battles-t61981/?partner_id=5OA45ES&utm_medium=online_publisher&cmp=singapore-activities&deeplink_id=009b7848-4283-5208-a378-11e081973431&page_id=ccae3146-853d-5676-b54a-aa296c4ed2af), as I learned so much about the country’s history!

## Get Lost in the Malay-Muslim Vibes of Kampong Glam

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Kampong-Glam-Singapore.jpg)

Kampong Glam is the Malay-Muslim Quarter of Singapore

Kampong Glam is one of the most interesting neighbourhoods in Singapore, all concentrated around the enormous golden down of Sultan Mosque. I’m convinced it’s impossible to take a bad photo of this beauty!

This part of Singapore is easy to explore on foot.

Bussorah Street, Arab Street, and Haji Lane are the three main streets in the neighbourhood, but I loved that all three of them had their own distinctive feel.

Bussorah is the most polished and tourist-focused, with Middle Eastern restaurants lining the street. Arab Street is where you’ll find fabric shops that are full to the brim with silk and textiles. Haji Lane is the chaotic one: a narrow, colourful alleyway with independent boutiques, vintage stores, and hipster bars and cafes to linger in.

I recommend heading to Kampong Glam both during the day and in the evening, as the Sultan Mosque is even more impressive when it’s all lit up at night!

## Head Off the Beaten Track to See Haw Par Villa

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Haw-Par-Villa-Singapore-shipwreck-scene.jpg)

I couldn’t stop giggling as I made my way around the dramatic scenes at Haw Par Villa

One of the most interesting places I’ve visited in Singapore is Haw Par Villa. It’s not a place you’ll often find on tourist itineraries for the country due to its location (it’s roughly half an hour by metro from Central Singapore) but it makes for such a unique stop.

I couldn’t stop laughing as I made my way from one chaotic scene to another, loving how unSingapore the entire park was.

Haw Par Villa is filled with hundreds of statues depicting Chinese mythology, folklore, and moral stories, aiming to teach traditional values through extremely vivid storytelling. And by vivid, I mean graphic, and kind of hilarious.

The famous section is the Ten Courts of Hell.

This is a series of walk-through dioramas showing what happens in the afterlife according to Chinese Buddhist and Taoist beliefs. Each court depicts different punishments for different sins, and the scenes are… over the top but I adored them. One moment you’re looking at a giant smiling deity, the next you’re watching a demon being punished in elaborate detail.

If you’re in Singapore with kids, this’ll either be a place they’ll delight in or be terrified by; use your own judgement here!

One thing to note is that as of mid-2026, the outdoor parts of park are temporarily closed for renovations, but the Ten Courts of Hell (my favourite part) is still open. It’s still worth a visit, in my opinion, even if some of the areas are off-limits.

## Complain About the Prices at Raffles

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Two-Singapore-Slings-at-Raffles-Singapore.jpg)

When in Singapore! (It’s expensive but it has to be done)

It’s a rite of passage when it comes to travel in this country: drinking a Singapore Sling in its iconic birthplace.

Yes, it’s wildly overpriced. And yes, the Singapore Sling isn’t even the best-tasting cocktail. But I still think it’s worth hitting up the Raffles Hotel for the experience.

The drink itself was created in 1915 at the Raffles hotel by bartender Ngiam Tong Boon. Back then, it was designed as a drink for women, as it wasn’t yet commonplace for women to drink alcohol openly. The cocktail is gin-based, and sweet and fruity, and was specifically designed to look like juice rather than alcohol.

Spending time in the Long Bar is part of the experience, and the main reason why I recommend sampling the Sling in its birthplace. Crunch your way across the floor to your table, and if you’re anything like me, you’ll be alarmed at how messy the room feels.

That’s because part of the Singapore Sling ritual involves eating peanuts and tossing the shells on to the floor. Yep, this is the only place in the country where you’re actively encouraged to litter!

As I hinted, you’ll pay a lot for this experience: S$44 price tag as of 2026, but I think it was worth it to have a unique experience.

## Shield Your Eyes From the Gleaming St. Andrew’s Cathedral

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1050/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Exterior-of-St-Andrews-Church-Singapore.jpg)

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1050/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Interior-of-St-Andrews-Church-Singapore.jpg)

St. Andrew’s Cathedral feels a little out of place in Singapore, and subsequently had me feeling like I was wandering around a European city rather than Southeast Asia.

It’s free to enter the cathedral and you’re likely to stroll past it at some point while you’re in Singapore. It sits right near Fort Canning Park and the National Gallery of Singapore, so it’s an easy stop if you’re moving between the two.

From the outside, it’s hard to miss: bright white, immaculately maintained, and set back behind a wide stretch of lawn that feels surprisingly spacious for such a central location. Inside, it’s just as polished. A high ceiling, rows of wooden pews, and tall stained glass windows that add some colour to the room.

What I liked most about the interior was that there’s no over-the-top decorations like you usually find in cathedrals around the world. It was just a clean, simple interior that makes it a calm place to spend a few minutes.

You won’t need long here, but it’s worth stepping inside for a quick look, if only for the contrast. One minute you’re walking through the middle of busy, modern Singapore, and the next you’re standing in a quiet, almost European-feeling cathedral and replenishing your energy levels before you hit the streets again.

## The National Gallery is Home to a Secret Free View of Singapore

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Singapore-National-Gallery-rooftop-views.jpg)

You can head up to the Ng Teng Fong Roof Garden for free views of the city skyline

If you’re a fan of Southeast Asian artwork (or would like to be), Singapore’s National Gallery is home to the largest collection in the world. It’s also one of the most visited art museums on the planet, with more than 9,000 pieces spread across the space.

As you might imagine, the complex is enormous. The gallery is housed inside two former colonial buildings — City Hall and the old Supreme Court — which have been merged together, so you’ll find yourself moving between grand historic rooms and more modern, open gallery spaces as you explore.

Access to all of the rooms comes in at S$30, and I’d estimate you’d need to give yourself at least three hours to see it all.

One thing a lot of visitors don’t realise, however, is that you can still experience part of the building without paying for a ticket. The Ng Teng Fong Roof Garden is completely free to access and offers great views over the city skyline and Marina Bay. Even if you’re not a fan of art museums, then, there’s still a reason to visit!

## Hang Out With the Merlion

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Lauren-Juliff-in-Singapore.jpg)

With the two most famous structures in Singapore: Marina Bay Sands and the Merlion!

I’m not going to lie to you here: Merlion Park is super touristy. At the same time, the Merlion statue is such a beloved feature of Singapore that I couldn’t imagine visiting the country and *not* snapping a quick photo with it.

The Merlion was originally installed in 1972 and quickly became a national symbol, representing Singapore’s origins as a fishing village (the fish body) and its original name of *Singapura*, which translates to lion city (the lion head). It’s definitely a little underwhelming in size, and you’ll probably take the same photo as everyone else, but it’s one of the most recognisable scenes in Singapore, so you kind of have to do it.

The park itself is essentially a small promenade along the water’s edge, where people gather to take photos with the Merlion and admire the views of the Marina Bay Sands hotel. Don’t expect to spend more than a couple of minutes here before moving on to explore more of the city’s waterfront.

## Sample Kaya Toast: the Ultimate Singaporean Breakfast

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Kaya-Toast-Singapore.jpg)

My favourite kaya toast spot is Toast Box

Because have you truly been to Singapore if you haven’t sampled the country’s most beloved breakfast dish?

On paper, it doesn’t sound like a lot. Just two thin slices of toast, a dollop of green-tinged jam, and a slab of cold butter wedged in the middle.

That jam is kaya: coconut milk, eggs, sugar and pandan leaves, cooked low and slow until it turns into something closer to a custard than a jam with an almost caramel taste. Nobody fully agrees on where it came from, with some crediting Hainanese cooks who adapted the British habit of jam on toast while others trace it further back to Malay and Peranakan kitchens, or even to Portuguese settlers who arrived centuries earlier. Whatever the true origin, it’s been turning up on toast in Singapore’s kopitiams (old-school coffee shops) since the early 1900s, and shows no sign of going anywhere.

Order the full set and your toast will arrive with a saucer of soft-boiled eggs on the side, cracked open and seasoned with a splash of dark soy sauce and a crack of white pepper, plus a strong, sweet kopi (coffee) to wash it down. The eggs take some getting used to (as they’re barely set), and the whole plate photographs like a crime scene, but give it a morning or two and you’ll understand why it’s so popular.

There’s a bunch of cafes and chains where you can try kaya toast, with most locals arguing fiercely over whether they prefer Toast Box or Ya Kun. Most locals prefer the latter but I had a better version at the former.

## Shop ‘Til you Drop on Orchard Road

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Emerald-Hill-Road-Orchard-Road-Singapore.jpg)

The area where Emerald Hill Road meets Orchard Road is great for taking old-meets-new-style photos

Orchard Road is Singapore’s main shopping street, lined with a plethora of malls, hotels, and restaurants.

If you’re here to shop, you’ll be overwhelmed by the amount of stores in this area. For high-street stores, head to ION Orchard and 313@Somerset. If you’re after luxury shopping, Paragon Shopping Centre is the place to be.

I’m not much of a shopper when I travel, so I’d dismissed Orchard Road during my first few visits to the city. When I *did* finally get myself over there, however, I still found plenty of sites of interest.

In particular, I enjoyed wandering down Emerald Hill Road, which is lined with colourful historic homes, and made for some excellent photo-taking. Every building on this street was so beautiful that I ended up spending 15 minutes just walking up and down and admiring the architecture.

## Take Photos of the Beautiful Library@Orchard

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Library@Orchard-Singapore.jpg)

Have you ever seen a more aesthetically pleasing library?

*Note: the library is currently under renovations until late-2026, so do check if it’s re-opened before planning a visit.*

If you need a break from the chaos of Orchard Road, library@orchard is a surprisingly good place to hide out for a while.

Tucked inside a shopping mall, it doesn’t feel like a typical library at all. ## Spend a Day on Sentosa Island

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sentosa-Island-from-above.jpg)

Sentosa Island from the cable car ride to get there

Now, if you’re going to be travelling to Singapore with children, you’re going to absolutely want to spend a ton of time on Sentosa Island. If you’re childfree, like I am, one day will likely be enough.

There’s a lot going on here, but it’s fairly easy to break it down into a few main areas.

We’ll start with the beaches. Sentosa has three main stretches of sand, each with a slightly different feel.

Siloso Beach is the most popular of the three, with beach bars, volleyball, pumping music, and a more social, lively atmosphere. Palawan Beach is more family-focused, with calm water and chill cafes. Tanjong Beach is the quietest of the three, and generally the one that I always seem to find myself on.

For more of a lively day, you’ll find an abundance of theme parks to make your way around.

Universal Studios is the main attraction, and you could easily spend an entire day rollercoaster-hopping your way around the complex. I’m a big fan of the S.E.A Aquarium and spent a couple of hours marvelling at all the interesting sealife they have to see. There’s Adventure Cove Waterpark if you prefer to spend get in the water yourself, plus Mega Adventure Park, 4D Adventure Land, and so on, and so on.

I really enjoyed diving into the historical side of Sentosa Island, too.

One of the most interesting places is Fort Siloso: an open-air military museum built around a real WWII coastal fort. You can walk through the original gun emplacements, tunnels, and bunker rooms that were used during the Japanese invasion, with a mix of preserved structures and exhibits that explain what actually happened here.

There’s also the Fort Siloso Skywalk, which takes you up into the treetops on an elevated walkway to view the island from above.

One of my favourite hikes in Singapore is the one that leads to the Henderson Waves bridge

I was so sad that I had a cloudy day for my walk around the Southern Ridges!

Still, I really enjoyed exploring this part of Singapore; one that few visitors make time to visit.

You can think of this part of the city as a trail system that winds along forest paths, taking you to viewpoints and along elevated walkways. I had a lot of fun strolling around, taking photos of the views, and seeing where I ended up.

My favourite area is the Henderson Waves Bridge, which you can see in my photo. It’s a curved walkway that sits high above the forest while offering up views of Singapore’s skyscrapers that you’ll rarely see in photos online.

## Forget the Zoo: Singapore has a Night Safari

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Lion-Singapore-Night-Safari.jpg)

I was excited to spot a lion on my Singapore Night Safari!

I thought the Singapore Night Safari was such an interesting experience! This is the world’s first nocturnal zoo, located next to Singapore Zoo. As the name implies, everything here is designed around low light, moonlight-style viewing, and animals that are active after dark.

Think about it: how often do you wander around a zoo and struggle to see an animal because they’re sleeping? That’s rarely a problem here!

There’s two main ways to explore the Night Safari: a walking trail network and a tram ride that loops through different geographical zones. The tram is my favourite of the two options, as it takes you through habitats where you might spot Asian elephants, rhinos, deer, and lions. I felt like I was on safari while I was there!

The walking trails are where things get even more immersive. Paths like the Leopard Trail and East Lodge Trail take you closer to smaller exhibits and more nocturnal species, like flying foxes and civets. It’s quieter here (some people just take the tram ride and then leave) and the whole experience becomes more about listening and scanning the shadows than standing in front of enclosures.

I spent two hours at the Night Safari. ## Hit up a Hawker Centre (Or Ten)

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Maxwell-Food-Centre-Singapore-2.jpg)

Maxwell Food Centre is a popular place to eat in Chinatown

There’s dozens of hawker centres in Singapore, and if you ask a local where the best one is, everyone will give you a different answer.

Maxwell Food Centre is one of the most popular, so it’s generally where I send first-time visitors, located in the heart of Chinatown and packed with stalls selling everything from chicken rice to noodles, porridge, satay, and a lengthy list of items you likely won’t recognise. If you want to eat like a local in Singapore, the hawker centres are where you can do so.

The most famous stall is the Tian Tan Hainanese Chicken Rice (the blue stall on the right in my photo), which was made famous by Anthony Bourdain on his travel food show, No Reservations. And never again could you eat there without having the join the lengthy queue first.

In a city that’s as expensive as Singapore is, eating at the hawker centres is also a great way to cut down on your travel expenses, as you’ll rarely pay more than few dollars for a meal here.

## Admire the Murals Around Tiong Bahru

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Home-Mural-Tiong-Bahru-Singapore.jpg)

The Home mural in Tiong Bahru

If you’re keen to get away from the crowds of tourists in Singapore, Tiong Bahru is a great place to do so, with the added bonus of some truly impressive artwork.

The pieces are scattered around the estate, and it makes for a fun treasure hunt vibe to roam around in search of them all. Most of the well-known pieces were created by local artist Yip Yew Chong — like the one in my photo — and I love that he focuses on everyday Singaporean life in the past.

The good news is that many of the murals are marked on Google Maps, so if you don’t have time to wander aimlessly, you can speedrun your visit to the neighbourhood instead. You’ve got Bird Singing Corner, Pasar and Fortune Teller, and Home all marked for you to favourite in advance.

## Be Dazzled By the Colours of Little India

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1400/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sri-Thendayuthapani-Temple-Singapore-.jpg)

I love how colourful Hindu temples are!

Singapore’s Little India is such a bright and colourful place to spend a few hours — and it’s home to some incredible food, too! As someone who has spent months travelling around India, I love that you can get a sense of what it’s like in India here, but without having to deal with the downsides that comes with travel in that part of the world.

The main thing to do in Little India is walk. There aren’t a huge amount of tourist attractions here, but it’s the neighbourhood itself that’s the point of interest. You’ve got bright colours, the smell of incense and spice, beautiful temples, and so many delicious things to eat.

I recommend starting around Serangoon Road, where you’ll find gold stores with jewellery on display, flower vendors preparing bunches of jasmine, textile stores with fabric piled high, and grocery stores selling everything from lentils to curry leaves.

From there, you naturally drift into side streets where things get even more layered. You’ll pass small temples, people queueing for dosa and biryani, and stores selling religious items, bangles, and traditional clothing.

Food is a huge part of being here, even if you’re not planning a full sit-down meal. You’ll see banana leaf rice restaurants, thali sets being carried out on trays, and small dessert shops selling things like gulab jamun and jalebi. A lot of people end up eating more than planned just because everything smells better than expected.

One of the best stops is the Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple: a colourful Hindu temple that’s covered in detailed sculptures and bright figures, and I’d strongly recommend you take the time to visit.

## Ride the Singapore Flyer

![](https://sp-ao.shortpixel.ai/client/to_webp,q_glossy,ret_img,w_1500/https://www.neverendingfootsteps.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Singapore-Flyer-at-night.jpg)

Checking out the Singapore Flyer from Gardens by the Bay!

[The Singapore Flyer](https://www.getyourguide.com/singapore-l170/singapore-singapore-flyer-entry-ticket-t499726/?partner_id=5OA45ES&utm_medium=online_publisher&cmp=singapore-activities&deeplink_id=d2cc17dc-361c-593b-88d0-34f459e0d096&page_id=ccae3146-853d-5676-b54a-aa296c4ed2af) (S$46) is a gigantic observation wheel (one of the largest in the world!) on the edge of Marina Bay, with enclosed glass capsules that slowly take you high above the skyline. The whole ride takes around 30 minutes and once you’re moving, it’s smooth enough that you barely notice it.

As you rise up, the view opens up in layers. First, there’s the waterfront, then Marina Bay Sands across from you, you then get a full look at the city’s skyline, and then eventually get to see out towards the port, with ships scattered across the water. On a clear day, you can see surprisingly far.

Most people time their visit for sunset or early evening — and especially when the Garden Rhapsody is running at Gardens by the Bay! I enjoyed my visit during the day, however, as I felt I got to see more of the city at this time.
