## Asia

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Asia_banner_Rice_terraces.jpg/3840px-Asia_banner_Rice_terraces.jpg?utm_source=en.wikivoyage.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Asia_banner_Rice_terraces.jpg "Rice terraces in the Philippines")

**Asia** is the world's largest continent by land area and population, home to 4.6 billion people, which is more than half of the world's population.

The continent is too massive and diverse to conceptualize as a single digestible travel "destination". Travel options range widely, from the desert ruins and modern mega-malls of the [Middle East](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East") to the magnificent ancient monuments and giant mountains of [South Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), from the beach bungalows and jungle treks of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") to the mega-cities and technology capitals of [East Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia").

![](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:TajMahal.jpg)

The Taj Mahal in Agra, India

Asia gets a tremendous number of travelers, both for tourism and business. Different publications give different figures and things change over time, but all agree that many of the world's most-visited cities are in Asia. [One survey](https://www.en-vols.com/en/getaways/travel/most-visited-cities-2025/) had eight of the top ten for 2025 in Asia. Their list, in order, was [Bangkok](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bangkok "Bangkok"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong"), [London](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/London "London"), [Macau](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Macau "Macau") (mainly for gambling), [Istanbul](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Istanbul "Istanbul"), [Dubai](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Dubai "Dubai"), [Mecca](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mecca "Mecca") (mainly for the [Hajj](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hajj "Hajj") pilgrimage), [Antalya](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Antalya "Antalya") (Turkish beaches), [Paris](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") and [Kuala Lumpur](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur "Kuala Lumpur"), and even 10th place KL had over 17 million visitors that year.

Asia offers intriguing destinations for every type of traveller, be they a novice or an experienced road-warrior. Easier options include modern, prosperous countries like [Japan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") and the East Asian Tigers of [Singapore](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong"), [Taiwan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan"), and [South Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), where people enjoy very high standards of living.

There are also poor Asian countries with significant levels of malnutrition, such as [Cambodia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia"), [Afghanistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), [Laos](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Laos "Laos"), and [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste"). Some countries are well-established on the budget travellers' [Banana Pancake Trail](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Banana_Pancake_Trail "Banana Pancake Trail"), including [Thailand](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), [Vietnam](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam"), and [Indonesia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia").

There are countries that strictly restrict tourism to certain regions or types of tourism, such as [Bhutan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan") and the [Maldives](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives"). [North Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") and [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkmenistan "Turkmenistan"), easily the world's most isolated and repressive states, take it even further, with constantly-watched group tours dedicated to their "great leaders".

Of course, there are also many countries lying somewhere in the middle, which is where one might place the emerging powerhouses of [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China") and [India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/India "India"); those huge, populous lands make wonderful travel destinations in themselves due to their long history, traditions and diversity.

The traditional Asia–Europe border through Russia is the Ural Mountains and the Ural River. For cultural and historical reasons, some parts of the Caucasus are considered European. The Bosphorus bisecting [Istanbul](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Istanbul "Istanbul") is also regarded as the border between Asia and Europe. [Sinai](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sinai "Sinai") is geographically in Asia but politically a part of Egypt. And at the other end of Asia, the continental plate of Australia also includes parts of [Indonesia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), which is generally counted as part of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"). Similarly, the island of [Cyprus](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Cyprus "Cyprus") is geographically part of Asia, but usually considered part of Europe.

## Understand

Asia is the birthplace of eleven major [religions](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Religion_and_spirituality "Religion and spirituality"): namely, [Hinduism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Islam](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Islam "Islam"), [Christianity](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"), [Judaism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Judaism "Judaism"), [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism "Zoroastrianism"), [Sikhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"), Shintoism, Taoism, Jainism and the [Baha'i Faith](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Baha'i_Faith "Baha'i Faith"), not to mention countless other tribal faiths. Often called the **East** to distinguish from [the West](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/The_West "The West"), the continent is also home to the world's fastest growing cities, a wide range of exotic cuisines, the world's highest mountain ranges and some of the world's most ancient cultures.

### Geography

Asia is by far the largest continent and as such is extremely varied geographically. Asia contains virtually every possible climate and terrain from the frozen plains of Siberia to the jungles of Indonesia to the deserts of Arabia.

![](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Everest_North_Face_toward_Base_Camp_Tibet_Luca_Galuzzi_2006.jpg)

All summits above 7,000 m, including the pictured Mount Everest, are in Asia

Asia's (and the world's) highest point is [Mount Everest](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest"), in the [Himalayas](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") along the border of [Tibet](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet") and [Nepal](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), which rises to 8,848.89 m (29,031.8 ft) above sea level. Its lowest point is the [Dead Sea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Dead_Sea "Dead Sea"), located at the meeting points of [Israel](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"), [Palestine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Palestine "Palestine") and [Jordan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan"), whose surface is 400 m (1,300 ft) below sea level. Asia's longest river is the [Yangtze](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Along_the_Yangtze_River "Along the Yangtze River"), which runs 6,300 km (3,900 mi) through [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China") all the way from the high Tibetan Plateau to [Shanghai](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Shanghai "Shanghai"). Its largest lake is the 386,400-km² (149,200-square mile) Caspian Sea, which is surrounded by several Central Asian nations. Asia is bounded by the Pacific Ocean to the east, by [Australia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Australia "Australia") to the southeast, and by the Indian Ocean to the south. It is bordered by the Red Sea to the southwest, by [Europe](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Europe "Europe") and the Urals to the west, and by the Arctic Ocean to the north.

East Asia (China, Japan, Korea) is relatively temperate with distinct seasonal differences. South and South East Asia (Thailand, Indonesia, India, Burma etc.) has a monsoon climate. Generally hot all year round but with a wet and dry season. Most of Asia's rainforests and beaches are to be found here. West Asia (Iran, Saudi Arabia, Lebanon etc.) is hot and often dry. Winters can be mild but summer can be extremely hot. Central and north Asia have continental climate with the coldest winters outside Antarctica and hot summers; in some places the difference between all-time highs and lows can be over 100°C.

### History

The history of Asia is long, complex and diverse enough that some universities have whole departments devoted to it and a full account would be a multi-volume work. Even a reasonable summary would be much more than a travel guide could sensibly attempt.

## Talk

*See the general [Talk](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Talk "Talk") article for a discussion on how to cope without speaking the local language.*

![](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Signs_to_Lau_Pa_Sat_and_Merlion_Park,_Singapore_-_20141019.jpg)

Five languages in four scripts on signage in multicultural Singapore: English, Chinese, Malay, Tamil and Japanese

English proficiency varies widely between countries and regions. English is the main working language in [Singapore](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore"), and widely spoken as a second language by educated people in many former British and American colonies such as [Malaysia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"), [Brunei](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Brunei "Brunei"), the [Philippines](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hong_Kong "Hong Kong"), most of [South Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), the small Arab states of the [Gulf](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"), [Jordan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Jordan "Jordan") and [Israel](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"). Elsewhere, English proficiency is rudimentary at best. Refer to the guide for individual countries for how well you are likely to fare.

It is not possible to list all the languages and dialects throughout Asia. We can however detail the major groups you are likely to encounter:

- Sino-Tibetan – The Chinese languages ([Mandarin](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Chinese_phrasebook "Chinese phrasebook"), [Cantonese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Cantonese_phrasebook "Cantonese phrasebook"), [Minnan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Minnan_phrasebook "Minnan phrasebook") etc.), [Burmese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Burmese_phrasebook "Burmese phrasebook"), [Tibetan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tibetan_phrasebook "Tibetan phrasebook"), [Dzongkha](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Dzongkha_phrasebook "Dzongkha phrasebook") and some languages in [North-Eastern India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/North-Eastern_India "North-Eastern India").
- Turkic – [Turkish](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkish_phrasebook "Turkish phrasebook"), [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_phrasebook "Azerbaijani phrasebook"), [Uyghur](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Uyghur_phrasebook "Uyghur phrasebook"), [Turkmen](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkmen_phrasebook "Turkmen phrasebook"), [Kazakh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kazakh_phrasebook "Kazakh phrasebook"), [Kyrgyz](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_phrasebook "Kyrgyz phrasebook"), [Uzbek](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Uzbek_phrasebook "Uzbek phrasebook"), [Tatar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tatar_phrasebook "Tatar phrasebook") and several local languages throughout [Siberia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia")
- Semitic – [Arabic](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Arabic_phrasebook "Arabic phrasebook") is spoken in Arab countries throughout the Middle East, and [Hebrew](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hebrew_phrasebook "Hebrew phrasebook") is spoken in [Israel](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Israel "Israel").
- Indo-European – a huge group that includes most of the languages of Europe plus some in Asia
	- Iranian – a group widely spoken in the Middle East and Central Asia. including [Persian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Persian_phrasebook "Persian phrasebook"), [Pashto](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pashto_phrasebook "Pashto phrasebook"), [Tajik](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tajik_phrasebook "Tajik phrasebook"), and [Kurdish](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kurdish_phrasebook "Kurdish phrasebook"), and the [Ossetian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ossetian_phrasebook "Ossetian phrasebook") language in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus"), and some minority languages in [Xinjiang](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Xinjiang "Xinjiang"), China.
		- Indo-Aryan – the descendants of [Sanskrit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sanskrit_phrasebook "Sanskrit phrasebook"), including [Hindi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hindi_phrasebook "Hindi phrasebook"), [Urdu](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Urdu_phrasebook "Urdu phrasebook"), [Bengali](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bengali_phrasebook "Bengali phrasebook"), [Punjabi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Punjabi_phrasebook "Punjabi phrasebook"), [Marathi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Marathi_phrasebook "Marathi phrasebook"), [Gujarati](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Gujarati_phrasebook "Gujarati phrasebook"), [Sinhala](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sinhala_phrasebook "Sinhala phrasebook"), [Sindhi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sindhi_phrasebook "Sindhi phrasebook") and [Nepali](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nepali_phrasebook "Nepali phrasebook"), are found through much of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia").
		- [Armenian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Armenian_phrasebook "Armenian phrasebook") – the official language of [Armenia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Armenia "Armenia"), also spoken by the ethnic Armenian minorities in neighboring countries such as [Turkey](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), [Iran](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), [Iraq](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"), [Syria](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Syria "Syria") and [Russia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Russia "Russia").
		- [Russian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Russian_phrasebook "Russian phrasebook") – spoken in Russia and serving as a lingua franca in much of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus") and [Central Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"). Also the most widely spoken foreign language in [Mongolia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia")
		- [Portuguese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Portuguese_phrasebook "Portuguese phrasebook") – an official language in [Macau](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Macau "Macau") and [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste"), where it is spoken by a significant minority. It is also spoken by a small, mainly upper class, community in [Goa](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Goa "Goa"), while a Portuguese-based creole is spoken by the Portuguese Eurasian community in [Malacca](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Malacca "Malacca").
- Dravidian – a distinct language group of South Indian languages including [Tamil](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tamil_phrasebook "Tamil phrasebook"), [Kannada](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kannada_phrasebook "Kannada phrasebook"), [Malayalam](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Malayalam_phrasebook "Malayalam phrasebook") and [Telugu](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Telugu_phrasebook "Telugu phrasebook"). These are completely unrelated to the north Indian languages such as Hindi and Urdu, though they contain a number of loan words from Sanskrit.
- Tai–Kadai – [Thai](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Thai_phrasebook "Thai phrasebook") and [Lao](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Lao_phrasebook "Lao phrasebook") are spoken in [Thailand](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand") and Laos respectively. Other languages in this family are spoken by ethnic minorities in Myanmar, Vietnam and China.
- Austroasiatic – [Vietnamese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Vietnamese_phrasebook "Vietnamese phrasebook") and [Khmer](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Khmer_phrasebook "Khmer phrasebook") are spoken in [Vietnam](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Vietnam "Vietnam") and [Cambodia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Cambodia "Cambodia") respectively. Other languages in this family are spoken by ethnic minorities in Myanmar, Thailand and China.
- [Austronesian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Austronesian "Austronesian") – [Malay](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Malay_phrasebook "Malay phrasebook") and [Indonesian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesian_phrasebook "Indonesian phrasebook") are mostly mutually intelligible; most other local languages in Malaysia and Indonesia such as [Javanese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Javanese_phrasebook "Javanese phrasebook"), [Sundanese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sundanese_phrasebook "Sundanese phrasebook"), [Balinese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Balinese_phrasebook "Balinese phrasebook"), [Iban](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iban_phrasebook "Iban phrasebook") and Kadazan are not mutually intelligible, but also belong to the same language family. Most languages of the Philippines, the most important of which are [Tagalog](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Filipino_phrasebook "Filipino phrasebook") and [Cebuano](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Cebuano_phrasebook "Cebuano phrasebook"), are also in this group. [Tetum](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tetum_phrasebook "Tetum phrasebook"), the main language spoken in [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Timor-Leste "Timor-Leste"), also falls in this family. Other Austronesian languages are spoken in [Madagascar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Madagascar "Madagascar"), throughout the Pacific islands, and by the indigenous Taiwanese.
- Mongolic – [Mongolian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mongolian_phrasebook "Mongolian phrasebook"), as the name suggests, and some minority languages in Russia such as [Buryat](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Buryat_phrasebook?action=edit&redlink=1 "Buryat phrasebook (page does not exist)") and [Kalmyk](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kalmyk_phrasebook "Kalmyk phrasebook")
- Japonic – [Japanese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japanese_phrasebook "Japanese phrasebook") and the Ryukyuan languages spoken in the [Ryukyu Islands](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Okinawa "Okinawa") of Japan

Asia has a few significant isolated languages not proven to be related to others, including [Korean](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Korean_phrasebook "Korean phrasebook") and [Georgian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Georgian_phrasebook "Georgian phrasebook").

Japanese, Korean, the Mongolic languages, the Tungusic languages ([Manchu](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Manchu_phrasebook "Manchu phrasebook"), [Xibe](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Xibe_phrasebook "Xibe phrasebook"), [Evenki](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Evenki_phrasebook?action=edit&redlink=1 "Evenki phrasebook (page does not exist)"), etc.) and the Turkic languages share similar grammatical structures, which make the others easier to pick up if you already know one of them. Some linguists group them into a single language family called the **Altaic** or **Transeurasian** languages, but this is controversial.

## Get in

The [APEC Business Travel Card](https://www.apec.org/groups/committee-on-trade-and-investment/business-mobility-group/abtc) (ABTC) is a special type of visa that can be issued to business travellers from APEC countries (Australia, Brunei, Chile, China, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea, Peru, Philippines, Russia, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, Vietnam). The application process varies by country and can be rather onerous, but once granted, the card acts as a **business visa for all member countries** and grants access to fast-track immigration line (typically the one for crews and diplomats) at major airports throughout the region, for a period of up to five years. Canada and the United States are "transitional" members, meaning their citizens can apply and make use of the fast-track immigration, but do *not* get visa rights.

### By train

If you are coming to Asia by train, you'll likely be coming in through [Russia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Russia "Russia") or [Turkey](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkey "Turkey"), although other options may exist. For an interesting experience, try the **[Trans-Siberian Railway](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Trans-Siberian_Railway "Trans-Siberian Railway")**, or alternatively some of the lines from Moscow to Central Asia.

### By boat

Asian ports are cruise destinations, and various companies such as [Royal Caribbean](http://www.royalcaribbean.com/) and [Princess](https://www.princess.com/en-us/cruise-destinations/asia-cruises) run cruises from [Australia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Australia "Australia"), while [Holland America](http://www.hollandamerica.com/) travels across the Pacific from [North America](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/North_America "North America"). [Freighter travel](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Freighter_travel "Freighter travel") is another option, including if you wish to travel to ports in the Asian part of Mediterranean. Coming from Africa [sea piracy](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sea_piracy?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sea piracy (page does not exist)") is a threat to take seriously. Piracy in the Strait of Malacca has declined, but may still be relevant.

As the Bosporus is considered to be the boundary between Europe and Asia, one of the various ferries in Istanbul can also get you into Asia.

There is ferry access from Asia to Africa and reverse via Jordan and Egypt. As of 2019, ferries still connected African Egypt with Asia via ABMarine. However you will not be permitted to leave Sinai into the rest of Africa unless you take the Nuweiba, Egypt ferry from Aqaba, Jordan. Leaving Asia to Africa via Israel will leave you on Sinai, as you cannot get permission to go further using the Israeli borders or even the Taba, Egypt port. The Nuweiba ferry is the only way to go if you want to travel via ferry and land to Africa. If you enter Asia this route, several nationalities can enter visa-free (thanks of the AZEA Trade Agreement) as long as you exit Jordan via the same city within 30 days.

### On foot

Some cities, including [Yekaterinburg](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg "Yekaterinburg") are built across what is commonly considered the dividing line between Europe and Asia and you can comfortably walk across that line if you are in the city already. [Istanbul](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Istanbul "Istanbul") also straddles that border, but pedestrians aren't normally allowed at any of its intercontinental bridges and tunnels.

## Get around

### By plane

Air travel is a good mode of transport between this large region's many travel destinations. Fares are lower on average than in Europe or America, and [low-cost airlines in Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Air_travel_on_a_budget "Air travel on a budget") are rapidly expanding their networks particularly in Southeast Asia. In addition, Asia is also home to some of the world's best regarded full service airlines, such as **[Singapore Airlines](http://www.singaporeair.com/)**, Hong Kong's **[Cathay Pacific](https://www.cathaypacific.com/)**, South Korea's **[Korean Air](https://kr.koreanair.com/global/en.html)** and **[Asiana Airlines](http://flyasiana.com/)**, Japan's **[All Nippon Airways (ANA)](https://www.ana.co.jp/)** and **[Japan Airlines (JAL)](http://www.jal.com/)**, Taiwan's **[EVA Air](https://www.evaair.com/)** and the big three Middle Eastern airlines **[Emirates](https://www.emirates.com/)**, **[Qatar Airways](https://www.qatarairways.com/en/homepage.html)** and **[Etihad Airways](http://www.etihad.com/)**, which make good options for those willing to spend some extra money to fly in relative comfort with better service.

### By boat

![](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Godavari_river_ferry_and_bridge.jpg)

Ferry across the Godavari river in India

Ferry services connect [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China"), [South Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea") and [Japan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") in [East Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"). There are also very many international and domestic ferry services for the island nations in [South East Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_East_Asia "South East Asia") including the [Philippines](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), [Indonesia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), [Singapore](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") and [Malaysia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Malaysia "Malaysia"). Prior to the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine there were regular ferries between the [Russian Far East](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Russian_Far_East "Russian Far East") and South Korea and Japan; these ferries might resume once the war is over.

### By train

There are highly developed train networks in [India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/India "India"), [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China"), [South Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), [Taiwan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") and [Japan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), as well as less developed services throughout [South East Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_East_Asia "South East Asia"). Most of these countries, but especially China have also been investing a lot in [high speed rail](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_China "High-speed rail in China"), and even for longer distances trains might be a worthwhile option not only in terms of views and comfort but also in terms of price and speed, especially if you figure in the time and money saved while you sleep on sleeper services. In Russia and India, big parts of the country are mostly served by slow rail and it is a superb way to get into contact with locals (bring a phrasebook) or just see the country pass by the window. China and Japan are expanding their high speed networks, with China even planning to build international links to South East Asia and possibly North or South Korea. [South Korea's](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/KTX "KTX") relatively young network of high speed lines is also planned to expand within the next decades, however due to the political situation, international links are unlikely in the immediate future.

[Uzbekistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Uzbekistan "Uzbekistan") boasts the only high-speed rail in [Central Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), connecting [Tashkent](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tashkent "Tashkent") with [Bukhara](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bukhara "Bukhara"), and regular-speed trains connect most of the main cities in the country. Trains connect [Tajikistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tajikistan "Tajikistan") with [Moscow](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), but they pass through [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkmenistan "Turkmenistan"), which rarely if never grants transit visas for that route.

The so-called [Trans-Siberian Railway](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Trans-Siberian_Railway "Trans-Siberian Railway") connects European Russia with the Russian Far East via a few different routes: The Trans-Mongolian connects [Irkutsk](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Irkutsk "Irkutsk") and [Beijing](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Beijing "Beijing") via [Ulaanbaatar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ulaanbaatar "Ulaanbaatar"), while the Trans-Manchurian crosses northeastern China. A route also exists that connects to Vladivostok without leaving Russia at all, and there are also options that divert into [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan").

### By bus

Travel by bus is an option for the budget conscious traveler in most Asian countries, and often a good way to come into contact with locals. Quality of services ranges wildly from luxurious buses, on par with or even more comfortable and faster than some trains, to beat-up old school buses where you might conceivable actually sit next to a live chicken (hence their nickname "chicken bus").

### By car

As Asia has a high population density in many places and new road construction has not always kept up with growing traffic, congestion is a major problem, especially in urban centers. That being said, a car is often a good and sometimes the only way to explore more remote areas. Keep in mind that bringing a car might necessitate a number of forms and permits; you will probably need an [international driving permit](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Driving#Drivers'_license "Driving") and for some countries a [Carnet de Passages](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Carnet_de_Passages "Carnet de Passages") for the vehicle.

**Mainland China** (excluding Macau and Hong Kong) **does not accept international drivers licenses**. If you want to go to or through China by car, you need to either obtain a local drivers license or hire a driver; see [Driving in China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Driving_in_China "Driving in China") for details.

Land crossing between different countries in Asia is in some cases dangerous or impossible. Many border areas in Asia are either remote areas where the borders are open but access is dangerous, or in some cases the borders between countries are closed and have only been used for goods or humanitarian cases, certainly not for foreign visitors. For example, India's border with Pakistan is closed, and China's borders with North Korea, India, Nepal, and Myanmar are heavily restricted. For this reason, attempts by various adventurers to recreate the [Silk Road](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road") in recent decades without flights have always failed. Many other border crossings are open and safe, but you should research your itinerary in advance.

### Local transport

Many places have taxis, many of the major cities have good [urban rail](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Urban_rail "Urban rail") or bus systems, it is possible to walk or bike in some places, and some travellers will get around mainly on tour buses. While Japan has had world class (if expensive) urban rail for decades, China and some more unlikely countries have followed suit and are still expanding and improving vast light and heavy rail networks. Where those exist they are usually the best option for tourists and locals alike.

However in many places other options are available as well, and often these are what the locals mainly use. If "When in Rome, do as the Romans do" is part of your travel philosophy, then you should definitely consider these. In much of Asia there are various sorts of truck or van modified to carry passengers: *[angkots](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesia#By_bus "Indonesia")* in Indonesia, [jeepneys](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Jeepney "Jeepney") and [UV Express](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/UV_Express "UV Express") in the Philippines, *[marshrutkas](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Marshrutka "Marshrutka")* in the ex-Soviet states, [songthaews](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Songthaew "Songthaew") in Thailand, or similar vehicles elsewhere. In many places there are also pedal-powered or motorcycle-based tricycles, see [Thailand#Tuk-tuk](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Thailand#Tuk-tuk "Thailand"), [Philippines#By\_tricycle](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Philippines#By_tricycle "Philippines") or [India#By\_auto-rickshaw](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/India#By_auto-rickshaw "India").

Some areas also have motorcycle taxis, each with their local names (*habal-habal* in the Philippines, *ojek* in Indonesia, *xe ôm* in Vietnam). These are too risky and uncomfortable for many travellers, but some like them.

### Natural places

While all continents have a huge number of things to see, what sets Asia apart is the **incredible variety of things to see**. From the desolate [Siberian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia") tundra and taiga in the far north to colorful [Indian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/India "India") street scenes, from ruins along the [Silk Road](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Silk_Road "Silk Road") to glitzy, futuristic metropolises and from the camels in the [Arabian](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula "Arabian Peninsula") Desert to the [Komodo](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Komodo "Komodo") dragons roaming the jungles of [Flores](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Flores_\(Indonesia\) "Flores (Indonesia)"), it is safe to say that Asia has something for everyone.

Stretching from the Arctic all the way beyond the Equator, Asia is home to **virtually every kind of climate and ecosystem that exists in the world** and a corresponding number of different plants and animals. Almost all parts of Asia feature iconic animals, such as pandas, brown bears, tigers, monkeys, elephants, crocodiles, camels, and birds – though many of them are threatened in the wild. While north and central Asia is part of the [palearctic region](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Eurasian_wildlife "Eurasian wildlife"), south and southeast Asia makes up the [Indomalayan region](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asian_wildlife "South Asian wildlife"). There is also no lack of **amazing landscapes** either. Vast boreal forests cover the northern part of the continent interspersed by some of the longest rivers in the world. Further south, [High Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/High_Asia "High Asia") is dominated by an enormous system of mountain chains reaching from the [highlands of Turkey](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Eastern_Anatolia "Eastern Anatolia") across [Iran](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iran "Iran"), [Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") and [Central Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") into [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China") where it forks into a northern section including the [Altai](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Altai "Altai") mountains and continuing all the way through the [Russian Far East](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Russian_Far_East "Russian Far East"), and into a more southerly section including [Himalayas](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Himalaya "Himalaya"), which feature the world's highest summits, and then the highlands of Southeast Asia. Many mountains in the easternmost parts of the continent are [volcanic](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Volcanoes "Volcanoes"), and [Indonesia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") and [Kamchatka](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kamchatka "Kamchatka") are indeed great destinations for volcanic tourism. Central Asia features steppe, which turns into desert when traveling south, and if you've always wanted to experience a [desert environment](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Deserts "Deserts"), the Middle East is definitely a good choice. Another large desert is the [Gobi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Gobi "Gobi") at the borders of [Mongolia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia") and China. Finally, tropical Asia covering a part of East and most of [South](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), where not cultivated or urbanized, is dominated by various kinds of tropical forests and thousands of beaches ranging from secluded paradise islands like the [Maldives](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives") to some of the world's most popular beach resorts in [Thailand](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand").

### Cultures and religions

Home to more than half of the world's population, the birthplace of all major world [religions](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Religions "Religions") and using all the most widespread writing systems in the world, the **different peoples and cultures** you may encounter on this continent are likely even more varied than the landscapes. Almost all regions of Asia have ruins of **some of the [oldest civilizations](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Historical_travel#Asia "Historical travel")** in the world, often stretching back several millennia. The [Holy Land](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Holy_Land "Holy Land") of the Abrahamic religions can be found in the Middle East. Similarly, the Indian subcontinent is the wellspring of [Hinduism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Sanamahism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sanamahism "Sanamahism"), [Sikhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism") and Jainism, and East Asia of Confucianism, Taoism and Shinto. Asia also bears witness to the spread of the great religions in the form of amazing **religious architecture**, including the ancient tombs and houses of worship in [Angkor Wat](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Angkor_Wat "Angkor Wat"), [Borobudur](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Borobudur "Borobudur"), [Samarkand](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Samarkand "Samarkand"), the [Taj Mahal](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Taj_Mahal "Taj Mahal") and countless thousands of others. There are also other kinds of historical sites such as the [Great Wall](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Great_Wall "Great Wall"), [Petra](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Petra "Petra"), [Ayutthaya](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ayutthaya "Ayutthaya") and the royal and imperial palaces of both present and past dynasties. Even in the latter case the royal traditions may be experienced as in the royal change of guards at the [Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Seoul/Jongno "Seoul/Jongno").

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/St_Pauls_Panoramic.JPG/250px-St_Pauls_Panoramic.JPG?utm_source=en.wikivoyage.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

The ruins of St. Paul's Church in Macau, which shows the contrast between colonial architecture and the rapid development occurring in much of Asia

**Colonial influences** aren't as prominent as in Africa, the Americas or Oceania, but this doesn't mean that they aren't there. Virtually all of South and Southeast Asia were for centuries ruled by various European empires who introduced things like western architecture and foodstuffs (some of it from the Americas), as well as Christianity, European languages and the Latin alphabet. We should not forget Russian Asia as well as Central Asia which was part of the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union for a century and a half. **20th- and 21st-century history**, though for much of Asia featuring many wars and other conflicts, tyrannical regimes, oppression and genocide, are also present in memorials and museums. Finally if you want to plunge into **modern cities** with most the world's tallest buildings, glass, steel and huge LED-screens, extensive and modern transportation systems, and entertainment and amusement centers, Asia is the place to go too, especially the Gulf States and East Asia.

### Sports

#### Asian Games

The **Asian Games** are an international multi-sport event similar to the Olympics, albeit with participation restricted to Asian countries, as well as some dependent territories in Asia. The games were first held in 1951, and following that were held every four years starting from the 1954 games, two years apart from the Summer Olympics, and feature all Olympic sports, as well as several non-Olympic sports such as tenpin bowling, Asian martial arts and squash. Mind sports such as [Chess](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Chess "Chess"), [Go](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Go "Go") and [Xiangqi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Xiangqi "Xiangqi"), and even e-sports are also included.

The previous edition of the Asian Games was held in [Hangzhou](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hangzhou "Hangzhou"), [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China") in 2023 (one year delayed due to COVID-19), while the next edition will be held in [Nagoya](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nagoya "Nagoya"), [Japan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") in 2026.

## Eat

Asian cuisine is incredibly diverse, from Turkish kebabs to Arabic pita bread to Indian curries and Chinese noodles, there is really no shortage of different food that you can try. Rice, in its many varieties, is a very common staple throughout Asia. [Street food](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Street_food "Street food") is also available almost anywhere in myriads of variations. Southeast Asia offers a great variety of tropical fruit and wonderful food at inexpensive prices.

Wikivoyage has in-depth guides to various cuisines of the continent: see [Middle Eastern cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_cuisine "Middle Eastern cuisine"), [South Asian cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asian_cuisine "South Asian cuisine"), [Thai cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Thai_cuisine "Thai cuisine"), [Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine "Chinese cuisine"), [Korean cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Korean_cuisine "Korean cuisine"), [Japanese cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japanese_cuisine "Japanese cuisine"), [Filipino cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Filipino_cuisine "Filipino cuisine"), [Malaysian cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine "Malaysian cuisine"), and [Indonesian cuisine](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesian_cuisine "Indonesian cuisine"). Many parts of Asia have also developed their own unique takes on Western food that cannot be found in their respective areas of origin; most of these are covered in the respective regional cuisine articles, but see [Western food in Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Western_food_in_Asia "Western food in Asia") for an overview of these.

In East Asia and certain parts of Southeast Asia, chopsticks are used for eating. Although in hotels and restaurants with Western food and/or Western guests, cutlery will be an option. In South Asia, people eat with their hands.

## Drink

![](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Korean.tea-Daechucha-01.jpg)

Daechucha, a Korean tea

[Tea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tea "Tea") is the most common beverage, especially in both [South Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") and [East Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia"). In tropical areas, enjoy fresh fruit and coconut juices. Fresh sugar cane juice is readily available in many cities in India. It can be served plain or with ginger and lemon. In some areas, fresh water and clean drinking water may not easily be available. Yogurt-based drinks are common in some areas, *lassi* in [India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/India "India") and [Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") and *doogh* in some western parts of Asia like [Iran](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and [Armenia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Armenia "Armenia").

[Alcohol](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Alcohol "Alcohol") is widely available throughout North, Southeast and East Asia. Often the larger cities or tourist areas have a modern nightlife scene. Alcohol is illegal in some parts of India and in some countries in the Middle East. While beer is commonly available in the countries that serve alcohol it often uses rice as the basic grain which affects taste somewhat. Another common beverage - especially in Japan, Korea and China - is "rice wine" (closer to beer as it contains more starch than sugar) sold and produced under various names and with a range of variations on the basic recipe.

## Sleep

Depending on where you are everything from a tent with only the most basic facilities (if any) to a full blown five star hotel is available. Prices tend to reflect that.

There are all kinds of traditional local lodging too: try a [ryokan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ryokan "Ryokan") in [Japan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), a yurt in [Mongolia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia"), a [caravanserai](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Silk_Road#Sleep "Silk Road") along the Silk Road, or a Bedouin tent in [Israel](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Israel "Israel"). Any of these is not just a place to lay your head, but a unique cultural experience.

## Stay safe

### Armed conflicts

Due to the vast size, safety in Asia varies wildly. It is a safe place in general and most tourist attractions on the continent are far from conflict, There are, however, some regions in which conflict and/or general lawlessness exists. The most obvious examples are [Afghanistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan"), [Myanmar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), [Yemen](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Yemen "Yemen"), and big parts of [Iraq](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"). Iraq's government controls only parts of the country and Afghanistan is in a state of continued armed conflict between the ruling Taliban and various other Islamist and rebel groups. There are full-scale civil wars in [Myanmar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") and [Yemen](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Yemen "Yemen"). These countries are considered no-go areas and should be completely avoided by travelers (if you absolutely must go, consult [War zone safety](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/War_zone_safety "War zone safety") and the authorities of your country beforehand).

Although much of the [Middle East](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East") can be visited without any major risks, it is known for its political tensions and there are no-go areas, for the moment especially Yemen. As of 2026, the [Gaza Strip](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip "Gaza Strip") is a war zone between Palestinian factions and the Israeli army, and kidnappings of foreigners have occurred. [Israel](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Israel "Israel") has coped with missile attacks as well as suicide bombings by radical militant groups, such as Hamas and Hezbollah, but mostly in areas close to the Gaza Strip and Lebanon (and attacks from Lebanon happen only occasionally), and by far the biggest threat in most of the country is from dangerous drivers. [Lebanon](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon") and the [West Bank](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/West_Bank "West Bank") (Palestinian National Authority) generally cope with an unstable political situation and internal conflict. As of July 2026, [Iran](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") is fighting a war with the United States, and both civilians and the Gulf States are getting caught in the crossfire. [Syria](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"), while no longer in a full-blown civil war, is still in a very fragile state, and there are periodic attacks in rural, majority-minority regions of the country.

Some regions of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Caucasus "Caucasus") are considered dangerous due to active insurgent groups, particularly the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/North_Caucasus "North Caucasus") ([Chechnya](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Chechnya "Chechnya")), [Abkhazia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Abkhazia "Abkhazia") and [South Ossetia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Ossetia "South Ossetia").

[Central Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia") is by and large a very safe place (barring Afghanistan). While the region had bad Islamist attacks in the 1990s, those are a thing of the past, and now the greatest threats revolve around inter-ethnic conflict, usually between the government and an ethnic minority region. The [Pamir](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pamirs "Pamirs") region in particular tends to flare up every so often, and the Tajik government requires permits to access the region and can close it to foreigners at any time.

[Bahrain](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bahrain "Bahrain") has experienced some political violence in the past, so check on current conditions before you go.

More dangerously, [Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") is active in a full-scale war in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa") and the [Federally Administered Tribal Areas](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Federally_Administered_Tribal_Areas "Federally Administered Tribal Areas"), both of which should not be visited. [Kashmir](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir"), claimed by both Pakistan and India, is also a region with tens of thousands of casualties since 1989 due to political strife and insurgency. There have been various insurgencies in India like a Maoist and Khalistani insurgency (now non-existent following operations by security forces), [Northeastern India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Northeastern_India "Northeastern India") copes with a low level insurgency, some of which have armed factions. There also may be tribal conflict from time to time there. The safest areas of Indian for all travelers including women traveling solo are in the Western Himalayan states of [Himachal Pradesh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Himachal_Pradesh "Himachal Pradesh") and [Uttarakhand](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Uttarakhand "Uttarakhand"). All countries in [South Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia") with the exception of [Bhutan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan"), face intermittent protests and riots in anger with the government.

[Southeast Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia") is a major travel region and most of it is perfectly safe to visit. A notable exception is [Myanmar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), which has longstanding ethnic and political conflicts, and is in a state of civil war, particularly in the border regions. But even in some popular countries, there are some areas that should be avoided. [Bangsamoro](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bangsamoro "Bangsamoro"), a Muslim autonomous region in [Mindanao](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mindanao "Mindanao") in the south of the [Philippines](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines"), is an area of conflict between the government and the Muslim separatist movements. The rest of the country is safe. [Thailand](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), the most visited country in Southeast Asia, is generally safe with the notable exception of [four deep southern provinces](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southern_Gulf_Coast "Southern Gulf Coast"), where fighting between the Thai military and Islamic insurgent groups still continues, though tourists are rarely if ever the targets of violence. Cambodia and Thailand have had some border trouble and it is advised not to travel there. [Indonesia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") is a very diverse country, with armed groups fighting for independence in [Papua](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Papua "Papua"), while [Maluku](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Maluku "Maluku") and [Aceh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Aceh "Aceh") are relatively safe now. Some countries, like Cambodia, Vietnam, and Laos, still have landmines leftover from wars in the 20th century. While de-mining efforts have been ongoing, the further off the marked trails you get, the greater the risk of coming across an unexploded landmine.

[East Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/East_Asia "East Asia") is generally the safest area in the continent for visitors, but political tensions also exist in this region. You should be aware that [North Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea") and [South Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea") are still officially at war and hostilities could in theory resume at any time, although the [border between them](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/DMZ_\(Korea\) "DMZ (Korea)") is still a worthwhile tourist destination in its own right (check current conditions if you plan a visit). Some visitors have also been arbitrarily arrested in North Korea, and less often, China. [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China"), a vast and diverse country, is a very safe destination with regard to violent crime, yet the restive provinces of [Tibet](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet") and [Xinjiang](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Xinjiang "Xinjiang") still often bar foreigners at any hint of trouble. China has low level border disputes with India which has a risk of escalation.

### Crime

Refer to the specific country pages and the authorities of your country for more information. As a precaution, always know the address of embassies, and notify in case of any issues faced.

### Natural disasters

Being the largest continent by far, practically every thinkable natural disaster is a risk at least somewhere in Asia. The area approximately along the Silk road is prone to [earthquakes](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Earthquakes "Earthquakes"), and areas along the Pacific "Ring of Fire" — [Kamchatka](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kamchatka "Kamchatka"), [Japan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), the [Philippines](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Philippines "Philippines") and [Indonesia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") — have both [volcanoes](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Volcanoes "Volcanoes") and quakes. Especially in South and Southeast Asia monsoon rains often result in massive floods. Tropical storms ([typhoons](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Typhoons "Typhoons") and cyclones) are a risk in much of tropical Asia during Northern Hemisphere summer and autumn. Smog and dust storms often occur especially in the northern part of China, making it very unpleasant for people with breathing difficulties.

## Stay healthy

Many areas of Asia, especially Southeast Asia and South Asia, are humid tropical, and there are health risks attached to travel in those regions. See [tropical diseases](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tropical_diseases "Tropical diseases") and the country articles for specific information.

Parts of Asia, such as [Mongolia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mongolia "Mongolia") and [Siberia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Siberia "Siberia"), have extremely harsh winters. See [cold weather](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Cold_weather "Cold weather") and country or region articles for details.

In some areas, such as [Tibet](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet"), travelers will need to take precautions against [altitude sickness](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Altitude_sickness "Altitude sickness").

## Respect

### Religion

Especially in the [Middle East](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East"), [South Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"), and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia"), people take their [religion](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Religion_and_spirituality "Religion and spirituality") very seriously, and the rules at places of worship and holy places are to be followed. Considerations include coming to these buildings in modest clothing, taking pictures only where allowed, and not touching things that are considered sacred. The sanctions can be heavy.

### Dress code

It is impossible to generalize about a dress code for all of Asia, but you will never go wrong by dressing modestly, which in most places means to wear pants that cover the knee and shirts with sleeves. Some particularly strict Muslim countries outright forbid immodest dress, which may require women to wear veils. In some regions where this isn't the law, locals may still look askance at women wearing short shorts and tank tops.

## Connect

Some of the world's best and cheapest internet connectivity is to be found in parts of Asia, with extremely fast speeds in [South Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea"), [Japan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Japan "Japan"), [Singapore](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore "Singapore") and many other countries on both fixed line and mobile. Conversely many countries also lag behind, and connections may be hard to come by. See country and region articles.

Censorship is a big issue for connectivity in a handful of countries including [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China"), [North Korea](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/North_Korea "North Korea"), [Myanmar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") and [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkmenistan "Turkmenistan"), where many websites and social networks are actively blocked from use.
