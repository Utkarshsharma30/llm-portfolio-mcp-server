## South Asia

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c8/Ploughing_Fields_crop_banner.jpg?utm_source=en.wikivoyage.org&utm_campaign=index&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Ploughing_Fields_crop_banner.jpg "Rice fields in Bangladesh")

**South Asia**, also known as the **Indian subcontinent** (or "the subcontinent"), is a triangular landmass bordered by the [Himalayas](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") in the north, the Indian Ocean in the south, and the Ganges and Indus river valleys in east and west. Home to 2.08 billion people, South Asia has the world's tallest mountains, and is the origin of some of the world's oldest civilisations and the wellspring of [Hinduism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Sanamahism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sanamahism "Sanamahism"), [Sikhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"), and Jainism.

### Associated destinations

The following destinations are sometimes considered part of South Asia, although they are categorised elsewhere in Wikivoyage:

- [Afghanistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") can be considered part of the region; although it is mostly considered to be part of [Central Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Central_Asia "Central Asia"), it often involves itself in South Asian regional organisations.
- The [British Indian Ocean Territory](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/British_Indian_Ocean_Territory "British Indian Ocean Territory") is a group of islands administered by Britain, south of the Maldives. However, it is off-limits for most people.
- [Iran](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") is occasionally considered part of the region, but more often considered to be in the [Middle East](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Middle_East "Middle East") or West Asia.
- [Myanmar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") (formerly Burma) may be considered part of South Asia due to its history as part of [British India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"), but it is usually considered part of [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia").
- [Tibet](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet") is sometimes considered to be part of South Asia, and shares close cultural ties with Nepal, Bhutan, [Ladakh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ladakh "Ladakh"), [Sikkim](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sikkim "Sikkim") and [North-Eastern India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/North-Eastern_India "North-Eastern India").

## Understand

The Maya Devi temple in Lumbini, built on the spot where Buddha was born

### History

South Asia is the origin of four major religions: namely, [Hinduism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Sikhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"), and Jainism. [Islam](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") was introduced by invading Arabs starting around the 7th century and rose to prominence during the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire"). There are also ancient Jewish and Christian communities in India that date back some 2,000 years, and there has been a Parsi (Zoroastrian) presence in the Subcontinent since at least the 10th century. An additional layer of South Asian cultural unification derives from the influence of British culture, and especially the frequent and growing use of the English language, as a result of India having formed the " [Jewel in the Crown](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj") " of the British Empire.

Unfortunately, the Jewel was partitioned into the Hindu-majority state of India and the Muslim-majority state of [Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") in 1947, and the orgy of Hindu-Muslim bloodletting that followed the partition led to the deaths of at least half a million and the migration of 12–14 million people. The two new countries have fought four wars, three of them over the status of [Kashmir](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir"). The third war in 1971 resulted in East Pakistan becoming [Bangladesh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh").

The British ruled the island of [Ceylon](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka#History "Sri Lanka") and the [Maldives](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives") separately from India, and they became independent in 1948 and 1965, respectively. Ceylon became a republic as Sri Lanka in 1972, while the monarchy of the Maldives was abolished in 1968.

[Nepal](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal") and [Bhutan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan") were never formally colonised by the British, but were nevertheless under British suzerainty during the age of colonialism. Nepal's Gurkhas provided soldiers to the British Army throughout the colonial age, and continue to do so today. In addition, there are Gurkha regiments in the Indian and Bruneian armies, as well as a Gurkha unit in the Singapore Police Force. Bhutan had its foreign policy controlled by the British throughout the colonial era, a role that was passed on to India following Indian independence.

### Climate

Apart from the Himalayas, the climate is tropical, with monsoon in summer and dry winter. However, you have the extremes of this climate, i.e. in western [Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), monsoon is quite non-existent, and in [Southern India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Southern_India "Southern India"), it lasts for six months. [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") even has two monsoons, one in May, one in October/November.

### People

South Asia is one of the world's most densely populated regions. About a billion and a half people, or roughly a quarter of humanity, call South Asia home. The average population density of 305/km <sup>2</sup> (790/sq mi) is 7 times the world average. It is very common to find narrow alleys in many cities, with the buildings being wrapped in electric wires.

## Talk

The region does not have a lingua franca. However, as much of South Asia was under [British rule](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj"), and even the nominally independent areas were under some form of British suzerainty, **English** is widely spoken by educated people. [**Hindi**](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hindi_phrasebook "Hindi phrasebook") and [**Urdu**](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Urdu_phrasebook "Urdu phrasebook") are spoken over much of [India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/India "India") and [Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"). As the two languages are mutually intelligible, if you have to learn one before visiting, pick one of these. Hindi will also help you in **[Nepal](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal")**, as the [Nepali language](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nepali_phrasebook "Nepali phrasebook") is quite similar, while Nepali is also the main language in some bordering areas of India such as [Sikkim](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sikkim "Sikkim"). [**Bengali**](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bengali_phrasebook "Bengali phrasebook") is another major language spoken in [Bangladesh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh") and [West Bengal](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/West_Bengal "West Bengal") and understood in some other eastern states of India. Other languages that straddle international borders include [Punjabi](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Punjabi_phrasebook "Punjabi phrasebook"), which is spoken in parts of the India-Pakistan border region, and [**Tamil**](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tamil_phrasebook "Tamil phrasebook"), which is spoken in southern India and northern [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka").

Other than these, South Asia has a fascinating diversity of languages. India, in particular, is home to hundreds, and Pakistan also has quite a few. In the major cities and tourist destinations, you will be able to get by with English with varying degrees of difficulty.

It is very rare for a young local in South Asia to be conversant in any Western European language other than English. Some of the elderly in [Pondicherry](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pondicherry "Pondicherry") who grew up during French colonial rule (French India) still know **[French](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/French_phrasebook "French phrasebook")**, but this number is dwindling nowadays, and in the recent years, English has replaced French as the common second language in Pondicherry. You may also notice elderly **[Portuguese](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Portuguese_phrasebook "Portuguese phrasebook")** speakers in [Goa](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Goa "Goa") (who have grown up during Portuguese colonial rule over Goa, especially pre-1961).

### Overland

Getting in overland to South Asia can be a challenging task. From the west it's relatively easy, especially from [Istanbul, Turkey to New Delhi, India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Istanbul_to_New_Delhi_overland "Istanbul to New Delhi overland") (also known as Hippie Trail).

In the northwest, South Asia borders [Afghanistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") which has been a war zone for several decades. Entry from [China](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/China "China") is possible either along the [Karakoram Highway](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Karakoram_Highway "Karakoram Highway") or to [Nepal](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"), though you will need a special permit to travel through [Tibet](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet"). Quite a lot of bureaucracy is reportedly required to travel overland from [Myanmar](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar").

The number of direct flights between India and the U.S. and the U.K. are increasing.

### By boat

Some cruise ships visit the region. It is also possible to enter on smaller vessels, but make sure to do your research thoroughly before planning a trip, because part of the region overlaps with the area frequented by Somali pirates. An encounter with them is to be avoided at all costs.

## Get around

### By train

Although most of the region was under the British rule, the rail transport in South Asia varies by country. [India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/India "India") has the most extensive rail network in South Asia (see [Rail travel in India](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Rail_travel_in_India "Rail travel in India")), while the rail network of [Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") is mostly underdeveloped. Sri Lanka's train network is efficient and cheap, and includes routes with beautiful hill country and tea plantation scenery. [Bangladesh](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh") is famous for its overcrowded trains, while the Maldives, Nepal, and Bhutan have no trains at all.

### Nature

With the world's tallest mountains, as well as coastal regions with the world's highest rainfall, South Asia's nature is dramatic, but pressured by demographic and economic growth. South Asia is part of the [Indomalayan wildlife region](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asian_wildlife "South Asian wildlife"), with iconic species such as Asian elephants, tigers and peacocks.

Especially [Nepal](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal") and [Kashmir](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir") are world renowned for [mountaineering](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Mountaineering "Mountaineering").

### Religion and spirituality

South Asian religious movements such as [Hinduism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Buddhism](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") have a long history, and an intercontinental appeal since the 20th century. Many visitors come to South Asia to learn yoga, a group of physical, mental and spiritual practices from ancient India.

## Buy

For most countries in the region, [bargaining](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bargaining "Bargaining") is essential while shopping.

Both India and Pakistan have a long history of [carpet](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Carpets#Indian_subcontinent "Carpets") manufacture. The region produces a huge range of other arts and crafts items, often at attractive prices because of low labour cost. See the country articles for details.

## Eat

![](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Bengali_Fish_meal.jpg)

A Bengali fish meal

You will not be able to experience the best of South Asian culture until you have consumed its flavourful food. India alone has some of the best cuisines in the world; however, you can find something that will suit your taste buds anywhere throughout the region.

## Drink

Tea (chai) is a common beverage consumed throughout the region. In addition, while alcohol is banned or restricted in Muslim communities and some Hindu-majority areas of India such as Gujarat, fruit juices are ubiquitous.

## Stay safe

Much of the region is considered a safe place to visit, with the exceptions of [Northwest Pakistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Northwest_Pakistan "Northwest Pakistan") close to the border of [Afghanistan](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Afghanistan "Afghanistan") and parts of [Kashmir](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir"). Northwest Pakistan is a war-zone and is often controlled by extremist militant groups such as Tehreek-e-Taliban; it should be avoided as much as possible.
