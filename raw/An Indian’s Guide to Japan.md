My full Japan itinerary for 2025: Hotels, restaurants — the works

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*Ih7EhLRp5UGA0aZ57MP5-Q.jpeg)

Kyoto’s Arashiyama Bamboo Forest. Photo: Aditi Ahuja

For the longest time, my dream destination had been Italy. An endless supply of gelato, visiting the birthplace of pizza, drenching myself in bright sunshine at the plazas, seeing the country’s incredible history, grandeur, and so much more was at the top of my bucket list. But as it turns out, life had other plans and I ended up rerouting, and planning a visit to Japan instead!

I had never in my life imagined that I would travel to Japan before going to Italy. But a gentle *(read: strong!)* suggestion from my mother nudged me and my husband to plan an 11-day sojourn to the archipelago. We embarked on a nice, long vacation to Japan with our family.

The timing couldn’t have been more perfect — it was a much-needed break for me after long months of working on a huge project.

And Japan exceeded our expectations in every way possible that no other country could have, giving us a truly memorable experience.

**The Pre-Work: Things To Keep In Mind**

Since Japan isn’t a country that many people in my family and friends circle have explored yet, here are some important things to know before you travel or while planning your travel:

1. The **eVISA for Indians is FREE!** As per the [latest rules](https://www.mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa/visaonline.html), you do not need to pay anything as visa fees while travelling to Japan. And the process is much smoother, quicker and requires far less documentation than that for going to Europe, UK or USA.
2. For places to visit — you may find a plethora of information online, but you should **only trust Japan’s** [**official tourism site**](https://www.japan.travel/en/world-heritage/) (or personally curated first-hand guides like mine) for critical decisions.
3. **Travel light** if possible. Carry a medium-sized suitcase instead of a large one, as you will have to wheel it yourself in most cases — be it at the railway stations or to your hotel rooms. Backpacks would be ideal.
4. No, **you don’t have to learn Japanese.** You’ll do just fine without it. Most tourist spots have information in English too. But make sure you have Google Translate on your phone ready in case of any issues.
5. If you are a vegetarian, especially from India, make sure to **carry some packets of ready-to-eat food.** Overall, vegan options are available but you would need to do some research in the area you are living in to find vegan-friendly restaurants. I have listed a couple in the itinerary as well.

Travel planning is fun and interesting, as long as you ideate and execute it right. Here are the three phases or parts of the process of planning to visit Japan, peppered with some useful first-hand tips.

**Phase 1: Booking Flights**

Luckily, several airline operators run direct flights from India to Japan. Being in Delhi, it was easy to book an evening flight to Tokyo’s Haneda airport. You could also travel directly to Osaka if that suits your plan and budget.

Our **Japan Airlines (JAL) flight from Delhi to Tokyo** took around 7.5–8 hours to cover the distance and was quite comfortable. We went for a direct booking from the airline’s [website](https://www.jal.co.jp/in/en/) which gave us good round-trip prices, the option to choose our meal preferences and seats in advance, and also a decent night’s sleep. The food was well-prepared and in-flight entertainment too did not disappoint: neither for me, nor for my six-year-old niece.

**Phase 2: Hotel Reservations**

While all may seem hunky dory till now, the hotel reservation part may bring down your spirit. Prices of hotels in Japan are much on the higher side. A room for a couple for a single night at a good hotel could set you back by anything between INR 15,000 to 20,000, especially during peak travel season which is in April and May. So make sure you budget well for this phase of your travel planning.

On our maiden trip to Japan, we visited the three most popular cities for travellers: **Kyoto, Osaka and Tokyo** in this same order. This is also known as the ‘Golden Route’ on Japanese tourism websites. Our criteria for booking hotels were:

- Good location: In terms of accessibility and security
- Comfortable rooms with enough space for two suitcases and a good room size (at least 20 square metres or more)
- Breakfast included
- Things to do nearby
- Easy to reach nearby airport/train stations

Here are the **names of the hotels** we booked in each city, with a brief description for you to consider:

1. **Richmond Hotel Premier, Kyoto Shijo:** A luxurious and spacious hotel at a good location, not too far from Kyoto’s railway station. Excellent rooms, wonderful hospitality, free drinks and amazing munchies during happy hours (!!!). Breakfast could have had more variety though.
2. **Cross Hotel, Osaka:** Right in the middle of all the action at Dotonbori and Shinsaibashi streets. The room size was good, there were lots of things to do nearby and you could even have soft serve for breakfast. Highly recommended.
3. **Rembrandt Style Tokyo, Nishikasai:** A small hotel on Tokyo’s East side, Rembrandt was really close to the Indian eating joints and Tokyo Disneyland. Room size was compact but alright for the price and breakfast had unlimited fountain soda for you to enjoy!

**Phase 3: The Full Itinerary**

**Day 1—Reach Kyoto**

Arrive at Tokyo’s Haneda airport early in the morning through the direct flight mentioned above. Take the airport shuttle bus at 8am to go to Tokyo station. Alternatively, you could take a taxi but it would be a bit more costly.

Take the Hikari Shinkansen to Kyoto at around 10:35. **You can book the** [**JR pass**](https://www.jrailpass.com/the-japan-rail-pass) **in advance so that your travel becomes simpler and cost effective.** We took the 7-day pass for around 50,000 Yen. The pass can be booked online but needs to be physically collected when in Japan.

If you don’t want to take the pass, tickets are also available at the train station itself. Tourist-friendly, English-speaking officers would be there to guide you.

Reach the train station in about 2.5 hours, take an Uber and check-in at the Richmond hotel in Kyoto. Although the official check-in time is 3pm, they are happy to check you in earlier.

Remember to stock up on the room and bathroom supplies you need from the reception itself. The rooms just cover the basics.

But they have a free nightsuit and slippers for you to enjoy!

**And keep your breakfast coupons safe during your stay.**

In the evening, head to **Nishiki market** for some colourful lamps, vibrant atmosphere and lots of good food.

Rest, relax and don’t forget to charge your camera for the exciting day tomorrow!

**Day 2 — A ‘Sensei’ of Kyoto**

After breakfast in the hotel, head to **Arashiyama Bamboo forest**. *(pic above)* You can take a cab or use the subway. Keep your camera ready as the forest is as picturesque as it gets! There are plenty of picture spots, and you may see a lot of girls dressed up in kimonos and pretty hairdos.

Head to Lugara Pizzeria close to the forest for an amazing Italian lunch with vegan options.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*J-bdv2v4JYXGArO8UfqfWw.jpeg)

Fushimi Inari shrine. Photo: Aditi Ahuja

Then, visit the **Fushimi Inari shrine**, known for its iconic bright orange gateways. Climb up till as far up as you can! The peaceful environment will give you a spiritual and healing experience. Stroke some cats, and just observe the quiet surroundings, take a deeper look within.

In the evening, head to Wagyu Gyumon Ramen for some incredible ramen including vegan options too!

**Day 3 — The Golden Kyoto**

Start your day with a yummy breakfast followed by a visit to **Kinkaku-Ji** shrine: the Golden temple of Kyoto. Soak yourself in its lush green variegation and stunning views. Calm your senses in the tranquility of its lakes. And of course, grab a souvenir or two for your loved ones back home.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*gzOzv_dyOta4jzJFOKMeLA.jpeg)

Kinkaku-Ji shrine. Photo: Aditi Ahuja

Once you are done, check out the **Nijo Ji** castle in Kyoto. Again a beautiful spot which captures the wonders of the imperial capital. If you have time and the interest, and weather permits, do also visit the castle gardens.

Head back to OC Burrito for an early Mexican fare and some truly delish grub you would surely relish.

Pack your bags and stay ready for check-out tomorrow!

**Day 4 — Reach Osaka**

Wrap up breakfast and check out yourself at the kiosks at the reception. In case you run late, they may charge you a bit extra.

Head to Kyoto railway station and take the Hikari line to head to Shin-Osaka. It would barely be a thirty-minute journey.

Take a taxi to **Cross Hotel, Osaka.** Check in to the hotel and keep your stuff.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/0*fcR3N5fPUOmk6RzO)

Dotonbori street in Osaka. Photo by Juliana Barquero on Unsplash

Once you are fresh, get ready for a weekend full of excitement! **Dotonbori street,** the most happening place in Osaka, is a minute’s walk from you.

Stroll through the market, making pit-stops at Onitsuka Tiger, Uniqlo and other Japanese departmental stores that you like. You can shop till you drop, click pictures of the vibrant atmosphere and fill up on delicious food.

The nightlife is also pretty amazing, so you can spend as much time as you like.

But get some sleep and don’t forget to charge your cameras and phones too!

**Day 5 — The city that never sleeps: Osaka**

Load up on the sumptuous breakfast at Cross Hotel. You can try their egg preparations, hash browns and soft serves. **But remember to wear proper clothes and not your in-room pyjamas and slippers! They would not let you enter as they have a dress code for their restaurant.**

Head to **Osaka Castle**, one of the most prominent landmarks of the city and an absolute must-visit. **If possible, purchase tickets online in advance** and take the elevator to the topmost floor for the excellent view of the entire Osaka skyline.

Head back to **Dotonbori/Shinsaibashi** area for lunch. One option is Dotonbori terrace, which serves great authentic Japanese fare and also has vegan options.

For dinner, you could try an all-vegan cafe called PIVOT Base Cafe & Bar which serves excellent ramen and Gyoza. Also, Cross Hotel has an excellent Aquarium-themed restaurant in its basement (the name slips my mind but the food is excellent).

All of this is at walking distance from the hotel. (*happy sighs!)*

In the evening, you could take a walk up to Shinsaibashi and also check out **Don Quixote** for inexpensive items to take home, including liquor, beauty products and electronic appliances to state a few.

Get some rest as we change cities again tomorrow!

**Day 6 — Reach Tokyo**

The third and last time you will be taking the Japanese bullet train, back to Tokyo. This time, it would be a three-hour journey as Osaka is further West of Kyoto. You could also take a JAL flight from Osaka to Tokyo, although if you have a JR pass it’s best to use that.

Reach Tokyo and check in to **Rembrandt Style** hotel at **Nishikasai**. Relax and take some time off travelling and exploring today.

For dinner, head to Tokyo Mithaiwala for some delicious Indian chaat. Their Pav Bhaji and Aloo Tikki are truly divine! And Jalebi as well.

**Day 7 — Disney time!**

Spend the day at Disneyland Tokyo today.**You can book tickets online on the day of your visit or a little bit prior, if possible.**

Children above 5 years will have to have a separate ticket. But the ticket is a bit less expensive than that for teenagers.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*M7Z_OH3Jk2PKxZjFZZzZ0g.jpeg)

Disneyland in Tokyo. Photo: Aditi Ahuja

Spend the entire day checking out some wonderful rides like the Jungle Cruise, Winnie The Pooh’s Honey Hut, rollercoasters and lots more. There’s so much to do for every age group!

Click pictures and grab some souvenirs. And don’t forget to see the Disney parades and shows, if the schedule fits in your timeline.

**Day 8 — Tokyo exploration**

Once you are ready to go after a hearty breakfast, head to the nearest metro station Nishi-kasai. The station office would assist you in purchasing a 24-hour or **48-hour Tokyo Metro rail pass.** Make the purchase online but get the physical pass from the office**.** This would help you cover Tokyo in the fastest and cheapest way possible.

Although the metro line may initially look complex, the subway map and app are extremely helpful in navigating it. Check it out [here](https://www.tokyometro.jp/en/subwaymap/index.html).

Head to Nihombashi on the Tozai line and take the Ginza line to deboard on Asakusa. Visit **Senso-Ji temple,** one of the oldest temples of Tokyo which is a short walk from the metro station. Try your luck at the fortune-teller machines nearby. Take lots of pictures and grab some knick-knacks for your loved ones. You can also try some delicious Japanese desserts here.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/0*v49Mg9yFpUyI_sSD)

Senso-Ji temple in Tokyo. Photo by Tim D on Unsplash

Next, enter the metro station again to board the Ginza line and deboard at the **Ginza** station itself. Head to the Cascade Resort Dining for an amazing lunch comprising drinks, salad, Vietnamese rolls and Udon noodles or Nasi Goreng at amazingly competitive prices.

Now, Ginza has a lot to offer especially high-end shopping stores. So you can refresh your wardrobe or gift yourself a new pair of sneakers. However, my recommendation is to visit the 7-floor store named **Itoya** which focuses exclusively on stationery. It’s like Disneyland, but for stationery lovers. Each floor is dedicated to a different section of stationery. Try not to drool!

**Day 9**

The last day in Tokyo was bound to be packed as there is so much the city has to offer. But we decided to keep it simple and only visit the iconic locations, while we took some time off to pack and shop.

Take the Tozai line again to Nihombashi from Nishi-kasai. Change for Ginza line, but this time, travel in the opposite direction towards **Shibuya**. Once you reach, head towards the Hachiko exit and get clicked with the legendary, faithful dog’s statue paying a tribute to the whose Richard Gere-starrer movie left us all teary-eyed.

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/format:webp/1*xhYHshabbs5X8vQmPGL9QQ.jpeg)

Hachiko statue at Shibuya. Photo: Aditi Ahuja

Once you cross the iconic Shibuya crossing, check out the **Shibuya Loft** for a wonderful array of home essentials that are a quintessential example of Japanese craftsmanship. For lunch, head to Shake Shack for a vegan burger, fries and yummy drinks.

Board the Ginza line again and interchange for the Hibiya line at Ginza station. Head towards **Akihabara** — the electronics hub of Tokyo where you will find literally every possible gadget under the sun at really competitive prices. The only drawback would be that the product box and instructions are in Japanese, so better not to go for a heavy-duty purchase.

Pack your bags, taking home some sweet memories of a beautiful country.

**Day 10**

Head back home to India via the morning JAL flight.

Reach the Haneda airport latest by 8:30 am.

There is not much to eat or shop at the airport so keep that in mind.

If you are travelling with kids, use priority check-in and security lines.

Fin.

### Full disclosure: Remember, this itinerary is just the tip of the iceberg when it comes to travelling to Japan.

### There are a lot of cities and areas here which need much more time to be explored. For example, Hiroshima, Okinawa, Mount Fuji, Nagoya, and Nara.

### We prefer our travels to be relatively relaxed, so we did not go overboard with our day trips and exploration and focused on covering the three major cities of Japan first.

### Also, since we went in the middle of May, we narrowly missed seeing Sakura or cherry blossom in full bloom.

### But yes, in my opinion, the country beckons a second visit super soon!
