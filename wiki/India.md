# India Travel Guide

*Source: [The Unbored Club - 50 Places to Visit in India](https://theunboredclub.com/blogs/places-to-visit-in-india-first-time-visitors)*  
*Added: August 30, 2026*  
*Nickname: "Incredible India" | Population: ~1.48 billion | Area: ~3.29 million sq km*

## 🎯 Essential Planning Advice

**If you are planning a first trip to India from abroad, do not try to see everything.** India is about three-quarters the size of the European Union and home to roughly 1.48 billion people, so no single trip covers it all. In two weeks, most first-time visitors comfortably see four to six places with real depth.

**Pick two or three moods that matter to you, choose one anchor region and a contrasting second, and travel slowly. That is the whole secret.**

Most lists of places to visit in India read like a race: tick the fort, tick the temple, tick the beach, move on. This guide is built differently. After years of helping international travellers plan their first trip to India, we have noticed that people remember how a place made them feel long after they forget its name. So we have grouped these fifty places by **mood** rather than map, because that is closer to how travel actually lands on you.

India is vast and layered. It is roughly three-quarters the land area of the European Union, home to more than a billion people and dozens of languages.

---

## 🗺️ Gateway Cities

Most overseas visitors fly into one of these international gateways:

- **Mumbai** - Best for West and Maharashtra caves
- **Bengaluru** - Opens up South India
- **Chennai** - South India access
- **Kochi** - Easiest entry for Kerala

A private car with a driver, arranged for a day or a whole route, is one of the most comfortable ways to cover ground, and it costs far less than a chauffeur would in Europe or North America. Trains remain the most characterful way to travel, with air-conditioned classes that are perfectly comfortable once you know which to book. Autos, sometimes called auto-rickshaws or tuk-tuks, are small three-wheeled taxis best used for short city hops, with app-based taxis as the simpler alternative.

## 🌤️ Best Time to Visit India

### General Rule
**Broadly, October to March is the most comfortable window for most of India**, and it is when the majority of these places are at their best. The winter months are cool and dry across the north and pleasantly warm in the south, which is why they are the **peak season for overseas visitors**.

Our month-by-month guide goes region by region for detailed planning.

### Seasonal Exceptions
**The important exceptions are the high mountains.** Ladakh, Nubra, Spiti and the far Himalayan Northeast are **summer destinations**, roughly late spring to early autumn, because snow closes the roads and passes for much of the year. The Valley of Flowers is the rare place that blooms during the monsoon, the rainy season from around June to September, when much of the plains are hot and wet. Kerala and the Western Ghats stay green year round but are heaviest with rain in the monsoon, which some travellers love for the atmosphere and lower crowds.

**Simple Rule:** Plan the plains and the south for winter, and save the high Himalayas for a summer visit. If your dates are fixed, choose the region that suits the season rather than forcing a region into the wrong month.

## 🗓️ How Many Places Can You Realistically See?

Fewer than you think, and that is the good news.

- **One week**: One region, done well, such as Rajasthan or Kerala
- **Ten days**: Two connected regions, or the classic north with one soulful addition
- **Two weeks**: Four to six places with depth, usually a north loop plus a southern or Himalayan week
- **Three weeks**: Six to nine places, north and south, or a single deep dive across several states

**Pro Tip:** If a place asks for effort to reach (Ladakh, Northeast), give it more nights, not fewer. The journey is part of the experience, and rushing it wastes the effort.

## 📍 50 Places Organized by Mood

### 🌿 Slow Down & Reset
*Green, quiet, unhurried places - perfect for start/end of trip or mid-journey pause*

These are the places that reset your pace. Green, quiet and unhurried, they suit the start or end of a trip, or a mid-journey pause when the cities have worn you out. The first time I sent a jet-lagged couple straight to the Kerala backwaters, they thanked me for a week. A night on a backwaters houseboat is the gentlest possible start.

1. **Alleppey and Kerala Backwaters** - A network of palm-lined canals and lagoons in southern Kerala, best seen from a converted rice barge called a houseboat. About ninety minutes by car from Kochi airport. One or two nights is plenty.
2. **Munnar** - Rolling tea hills in Kerala's high country, cool and misty even in the heat, around four hours by road from Kochi. Ideal paired with the backwaters for a gentle Kerala week.
3. **Coorg (Kodagu)** - Coffee estates and forested ridges in Karnataka, roughly five to six hours from Bengaluru. The quiet homestays here are some of the most restful in the south.
4. **Wayanad** - A green plateau of spice farms and low mist in northern Kerala, good for walking and slow mornings, about two hours beyond Calicut airport.
5. **Majuli** - The world's largest river island, on the Brahmaputra in Assam. Remote and deeply calm, reached by ferry after a flight to Jorhat. For travellers who want genuine quiet.
6. **Gokarna** - A temple town with a string of walkable beaches on the Karnataka coast, softer and slower than Goa. Best reached by train, or a short flight to Goa followed by a two to three hour drive.
7. **Tranquebar (Tharangambadi)** - A former Danish trading post on the Tamil Nadu coast, with sea walls, a small fort and almost no crowds. A poetic detour on a longer south India route.

### 🕉️ Spiritual & Soulful
*Oldest continuously lived spaces - no need to be religious to feel the energy*

You do not need to be religious to feel these places. They are among the oldest continuously lived spaces on earth, and something about the ritual, the water and the sound tends to move people who arrive expecting nothing. I have watched sceptical travellers go silent at the evening ceremony in Varanasi.

1. **Varanasi** - One of the world's oldest living cities, on the river Ganges in Uttar Pradesh. Dawn boat rides past the ghats (stone steps leading down to the water) and the evening aarti (fire and light ceremony) are unforgettable. Reached by flight or fast train. Two nights minimum.
2. **Rishikesh** - Where the Ganges leaves the Himalayas, a centre of yoga and quieter riverside ashrams. About a short flight or drive from Dehradun.
3. **Bodh Gaya** - The place where the Buddha is said to have found enlightenment, in Bihar. A calm pilgrimage town with monasteries built by many Buddhist nations. Reached via Gaya airport or by train.
4. **Amritsar and the Golden Temple** - The spiritual heart of the Sikh faith in Punjab, where a gilded shrine sits in a still pool. Everyone, regardless of background, is fed free in the community kitchen (langar), widely described as the largest free kitchen in the world. Reached easily by air or train.
5. **Madurai** - An ancient temple city in Tamil Nadu built around the towering Meenakshi temple, its gateways covered in thousands of painted figures. A window into living south Indian devotion.
6. **Pushkar** - A small town in Rajasthan wrapped around a sacred lake and its ghats, gentle by day and atmospheric at dusk. An easy add to a Rajasthan route.
7. **Tiruvannamalai** - A quiet temple town in Tamil Nadu beneath the sacred hill of Arunachala, drawing seekers and walkers who circle the mountain on foot. Deeply peaceful and largely undiscovered by overseas visitors.

### 🏛️ Speechless & Grand
*The iconic architecture and cities that stop you mid-sentence*

These are the grand ones, the architecture and cities that stop you mid-sentence. Many appear on every list of India attractions for good reason, but the trick is to arrive early, stay late and let them breathe.

1. **Agra and the Taj Mahal** - The white marble mausoleum needs no introduction, but it rewards a sunrise visit and a second look from the gardens across the river. Reached by road or fast train.
2. **Udaipur** - A city of lakes and palaces in southern Rajasthan, often called the most romantic in India. Slow, reflective and beautiful at golden hour. Fly in, or arrive by road on a Rajasthan loop.
3. **Jodhpur** - The blue city, a sea of indigo houses below the enormous Mehrangarh fort. The old town below the walls is a maze worth getting lost in.
4. **Jaipur** - The pink city and the classic entry point of Rajasthan. Strong on forts, crafts and living heritage.
5. **Khajuraho** - A group of intricately carved medieval temples in Madhya Pradesh, famous for their sculpture. Quiet, remarkable and reached by a short flight or train.
6. **Konark** - The Sun Temple on the coast of Odisha in eastern India, shaped as a colossal stone chariot. Pair it with the nearby seaside town of Puri.
7. **Ellora and Ajanta** - Rock-cut cave temples and monasteries in Maharashtra, carved by hand over centuries. Among the great achievements of Indian art, reached from Aurangabad, itself a short flight from Mumbai.
8. **Mysore (Mysuru)** - A graceful royal city in Karnataka with a floodlit palace and old market lanes, an easy and rewarding stop between Bengaluru and Coorg.

### ⛰️ Thrilling & Challenging
*High, remote, wild places - need careful timing and acclimatization*

For travellers who want altitude, movement and a little effort. These places sit high, remote or wild, and they need careful timing and acclimatisation. We always place them in a second week rather than a jet-lagged first day.

1. **Leh and Ladakh** - A high desert of monasteries and moonscapes in the far north. Leh town sits at about 3,500 metres, and the surrounding region climbs far higher, so the altitude is real. Fly in and rest for two days before doing anything strenuous. Open roughly late spring to early autumn.
2. **Nubra Valley** - Beyond Leh, over one of the world's highest motorable passes, a valley of dunes, cold rivers and villages. A day or two adds real texture to a Ladakh trip.
3. **Spiti Valley** - A cold desert of cliff-top monasteries in Himachal Pradesh, harder to reach than Ladakh and all the more rewarding for it. For confident travellers with time.
4. **Valley of Flowers** - A UNESCO-listed high Himalayan meadow in Uttarakhand that blooms during the monsoon months, reached on foot over a day or two of walking. A rare reason to travel India in the wet season.
5. **Tawang** - A remote monastery town high in Arunachal Pradesh in the far Northeast, close to the Himalayan border. Long to reach and unforgettable once there. It requires special permits, so plan well ahead.
6. **Andaman Islands** - A tropical archipelago in the Bay of Bengal with clear water and quiet diving, a couple of hours by flight from Chennai or Kolkata. A restful contrast to the mainland.
7. **Bir-Billing** - The world's second-highest paragliding launch site, in the Himalayan foothills of Himachal Pradesh, with a gentle Tibetan-influenced village below. Good for a soft adventure fix.

### 🍽️ Foodie Paradise
*Cities worth visiting for the table alone*

Some cities are worth visiting for the table alone, and eating well and safely are not opposites. These reward a food walk with someone who knows the lanes, and a willingness to eat where the queues are longest. A quick note for first-time visitors: eating well and eating safely are not opposites. Busy stalls with high turnover, freshly cooked hot food and bottled or filtered water make street food a joy rather than a risk.

1. **Lucknow** - The refined old capital of Uttar Pradesh, home to slow-cooked Awadhi cooking, delicate kebabs and courtly manners. An underrated stop for eaters.
2. **Kolkata** - The cultural capital of the east in West Bengal, generous with sweets, fish, street snacks and old coffee houses. A city that takes food and conversation seriously.
3. **Chettinad** - A region of grand merchant mansions in rural Tamil Nadu with one of the country's most complex spice traditions. Stay in a restored mansion and eat where the recipes were born.
4. **Ahmedabad** - A walled old city in Gujarat, with a largely vegetarian food culture of remarkable range and a fine morning heritage walk.
5. **Hyderabad** - A southern city where north and south meet on the plate, best known for its layered rice and meat dish, biryani, and its old-city markets.

### 🏝️ Gloriously Remote
*The India that guidebooks skim over - rewards effort with space, silence and encounters all your own*

For travellers who want the India that guidebooks skim over. These take effort, and that is the point. They reward you with space, silence and encounters that feel entirely your own.

1. **Meghalaya** - A green, rain-soaked state in the Northeast famous for living root bridges, grown by hand over generations, and some of the cleanest villages in Asia. Fly to Guwahati, then drive into the hills.
2. **Rann of Kutch** - A vast white salt desert in Gujarat that shimmers under a full moon and hosts a winter craft and music season, the Rann Utsav. Unlike anywhere else in the country.
3. **Ziro Valley** - A gentle valley of rice fields and pine ridges in Arunachal Pradesh, home to the Apatani people. Remote, cultural and quietly beautiful.
4. **Nagaland** - A hill state of proud tribal cultures in the Northeast, at its most vivid during the Hornbill Festival in early December, a ten-day celebration of dance and music. For the genuinely curious traveller.
5. **Sundarbans** - The world's largest mangrove forest, in the delta of West Bengal, home to a Bengal tiger population that has adapted to swim between islands, and reached only by boat. Slow, wild and atmospheric.
6. **Hampi** - The boulder-strewn ruins of a lost empire in Karnataka, part sacred site, part surreal landscape. One of the most magical places in the south. Reached by train, or a flight to Hubli or Hospet.
7. **Mandu** - A romantic hilltop of ruined palaces and pavilions in Madhya Pradesh, largely empty of visitors and lovely in the monsoon.
8. **Sandakphu** - At 3,636 metres, the highest point in West Bengal, walked over a few days for a dawn view of the eastern Himalayas, including Kanchenjunga, the world's third-highest peak. For fit, patient travellers.

### 🎨 Craft & Living Heritage
*Places where India's craft traditions are still alive*

India's craft traditions are still alive, practised in workshops and villages you can visit. These places let you meet makers, buy directly and understand where the beauty comes from.

1. **Jaisalmer** - A golden sandstone fort city rising from the Thar desert in western Rajasthan, still lived in, with camel rides into the dunes and nights under clear skies.
2. **Bikaner** - A less-visited Rajasthani desert city with a magnificent fort, old merchant houses and a quieter pace than its famous neighbours.
3. **Bhuj and the Kutch craft villages** - In Gujarat, a cluster of villages practising embroidery, weaving and block printing, some of the finest textile work in the country. A dream for anyone who loves cloth.
4. **Mahabalipuram** - A coastal town near Chennai in Tamil Nadu, with ancient shore temples carved from granite and a living community of stone sculptors.
5. **Orchha** - A quiet riverside town in Madhya Pradesh with cenotaphs and palaces reflected in the water, romantic and largely overlooked.
6. **Shekhawati** - A region of painted merchant mansions in rural Rajasthan, sometimes called an open-air gallery for its frescoed walls. Slow, faded and full of character.
7. **Bundi** - A small blue-washed Rajasthani town beneath a crumbling palace, with step-wells and almost no crowds. A favourite of travellers who like their heritage unpolished.

## ⚠️ Common First-Time Visitor Mistakes

The most common mistake is trying to see too much. A list of fifty places invites greed, and India punishes greed with long transit days and shallow memories. The travellers who leave most moved are almost always the ones who saw the least.

1. **Trying to see too much** - India punishes greed with long transit days and shallow memories
2. **Underestimating distances** - Two close-looking places may be a full day apart
3. **Ignoring season** - Arriving in Ladakh before roads open, or in plains at peak summer heat
4. **Booking jet-lagged first day at altitude or on long drive** - Ease in near gateway city first
5. **Eating nothing from the street out of caution** - A little guidance makes street food safe
6. **Skipping local guide at great sites** - You'll miss meaning you couldn't see on your own

## 🎯 How to Turn Fifty Places into One Good Trip

Start with feeling, then geography. Pick the two or three moods that speak loudest, note which places share a region, and let a natural route emerge.

A first trip might pair the **grandeur of Rajasthan** with the **spiritual pull of Varanasi**. A second might combine **Kerala's slowness** with the **remoteness of the Northeast**.

This is the point where thoughtful planning earns its keep. India rewards travellers who go **deeper in fewer places**, and a good local curator can turn a scattered wish list into a route that flows, with the right drivers, the right nights and the right people to open doors.

## 💡 Practical Things Every First-Time Visitor Should Know

This is the part we wish more guides covered, because it is what actually makes or breaks a first trip to India from abroad.

### Visas and Entry
Most overseas visitors need a visa, and many nationalities can apply for an **e-Visa online** before travelling. Rules and eligibility change, so check the **official Indian government e-Visa portal** and your own government's travel advice close to your trip rather than relying on older articles.

### Getting Around
Use **short domestic flights** between far-apart regions, **trains** for medium distances and the atmosphere, and a **private car with a driver** within a region. Most good trips use all three. Book **air-conditioned train classes** for comfort.

### Money, SIMs and Connectivity
Cards and app-based payments work smoothly in cities; carry some **cash for rural areas**. A local SIM or eSIM is cheap and keeps maps and taxi apps working. Hotel Wi-Fi is common but can be slow in remote areas.

### Food, Water and Staying Well
Drink **bottled or filtered water**, eat **freshly cooked hot food**, and favour busy places with high turnover. A little guidance makes street food safe rather than something to avoid.

### Etiquette and Tipping
Dress **modestly at religious sites** and remove your shoes where asked. Tipping is appreciated but modest: rounding up, or a small sum for drivers and guides, is normal.

### Arrival Day and Jet Lag
Do not book a jet-lagged first day at altitude or on a long drive. **Ease in near your gateway city**, then move.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the best places to visit in India for a first-time visitor?
For a first trip, most overseas visitors do well with a compact, well-known spine such as **Agra, Jaipur and Udaipur** in the north, or **Kochi, the Kerala backwaters and Munnar** in the south. Add one soulful contrast, such as **Varanasi or Rishikesh**, and you have a rich, manageable route.

### How many days do you need in India for a first trip?
**Two weeks** lets you see four to six places with real depth: one anchor region plus a contrasting second, without long days spent only in transit. **Three weeks** stretches that to six to nine. Adding more usually means seeing less.

### How do you plan a trip to India from the UK, US or Australia?
Fly into a gateway city that matches your region (**Mumbai for the west, Kochi for Kerala**), sort your visa in advance, and build a route around two or three moods rather than a long checklist. Use flights for long hops, trains for medium distances and a private driver within a region. A local curator can arrange the whole route for you.

### Is it better to fly, take a train or hire a driver in India?
It depends on distance. **Short flights** save time between far-apart regions, **trains** are the most characterful way to cover medium distances, and a **private car with a driver** is the most comfortable option within a region. Most good trips use all three.

### When is the best time to visit India?
Broadly, **October to March** suits most of the country, with the plains and the south at their most pleasant. The high Himalayas, including **Ladakh and Spiti**, are **summer destinations** from around late spring to early autumn. Always check conditions for your specific route.

### Is India safe and comfortable for overseas travellers?
The great majority of these places are welcoming and well set up for visitors, with good accommodation and easy access. Sensible precautions apply, as anywhere, and remote regions such as the **Northeast reward extra planning and local support**. Check your government's official travel advice before you go.
