# Japan – Complete Travel Guide

*Source: World Adventure Divers - Fun things to do in Japan*  
*Added: August 30, 2026*  
*Country: Japan | Region: East Asia | Nickname: "The Land of the Rising Sun"*

## 🗾 Overview
Japan is a country where ancient traditions meet cutting-edge technology. Famous for its rich history, stunning landscapes, and unique culture, Japan offers an incredible diversity of experiences - from the bustling metropolis of Tokyo to the serene temples of Kyoto, from the snow-capped peaks of the Japanese Alps to the tropical beaches of Okinawa. Considered one of the safest diving destinations in the world, Japan is particularly renowned for its ocean activities, especially in the southern prefecture of Okinawa.

## 🏔️ Top Regions & Cities

### Tokyo
The vibrant capital city, a mix of ultra-modern and traditional. Must-visit areas include Shibuya, Shinjuku, Asakusa, and Akihabara.

### Kyoto
The cultural heart of Japan, home to over 1,600 Buddhist temples, 400 Shinto shrines, and numerous UNESCO World Heritage sites.

### Osaka
Known as "Japan's Kitchen," famous for its food scene, friendly locals, and attractions like Osaka Castle and Universal Studios Japan.

### Hiroshima
A city of peace with the atomic bomb dome memorial and the beautiful island of Miyajima.

### Hokkaido
Japan's northernmost island, famous for skiing, hot springs, and fresh seafood.

### Okinawa
Tropical paradise with pristine beaches, world-class diving, and unique Ryukyu culture.

## 🌸 Seasonal Highlights

### Cherry Blossom (Sakura) - Spring (Mar-Apr)
- **Best Time**: Late March to early April (varies by region and altitude)
- **Top Spots**: 
  - **Tokyo**: Nakameguro River, Chidorigafuchi Greenway, Ueno Park
  - **Kyoto**: Philosopher's Path, Maruyama Park, Kiyomizu-dera
  - **Osaka**: Osaka Castle Park, Kema Sakuranomiya Park
- **Hanami**: Traditional cherry blossom viewing parties with food and drinks

### Autumn Colors (Momiji) - Fall (Oct-Nov)
- **Best Time**: Mid-October to early December (higher altitudes earlier)
- **Top Spots**:
  - **Kyoto**: Kiyomizu-dera, Tofuku-ji Temple, Arashiyama
  - **Nikko**: Toshogu Shrine, Lake Chuzenji, Kegon Falls
  - **Tokyo**: Shinjuku Gyoen, Rikugien Garden, Meiji Jingu Gaien
- **Unique Experience**: Mix of green, yellow, and red leaves in early season

### Winter - Snow & Festivals (Dec-Feb)
- **Snow Monkeys**: Jigokudani Monkey Park in Nagano - see Japanese macaques bathing in hot springs
- **Skiing**: Niseko (Hokkaido), Hakuba, Nozawa Onsen
- **Festivals**: Sapporo Snow Festival, Otaru Snow Light Path Festival
- **Illuminations**: Winter light displays in Tokyo, Kobe, and other cities

### Summer - Festivals & Beaches (Jun-Aug)
- **Festivals**: Gion Matsuri (Kyoto), Nebuta Matsuri (Aomori), Tenjin Matsuri (Osaka)
- **Beaches**: Shirahama Beach (Okinawa), Zushi Beach (Kanagawa), Jodogahama Beach (Iwate)
- **Fireworks**: Sumida River Fireworks (Tokyo), Naniwa Yodogawa Fireworks (Osaka)

## 🌊 Diving & Marine Adventures

### Okinawa - Japan's Diving Paradise

#### Kerama National Park
- **Location**: 30-60 minutes from Naha by ferry
- **Best For**: Snorkeling with turtles, pristine beaches, easy access
- **Main Islands**: Tokashiki (closest, 35 min), Zamami, Aka
- **Marine Life**: Sea turtles, tropical fish, coral reefs
- **Best Sites**: West coast of Tokashiki near Aaren Beach

#### Ishigaki Island
- **Manta Ray Diving**: World-class manta ray encounters near Kabira Bay
- **Best Time**: April-October (manta season)
- **Alternative Sites**: Kuroshima, Iriomote islands (winter months)
- **Highlights**: Manta rays all year round, clear visibility

#### Yonaguni Island
- **Yonaguni Monument**: Mysterious underwater stone formation, possibly man-made
- **Best Time**: January-March for hammerhead sharks
- **Diving Level**: Advanced (strong currents, drift diving)
- **Unique**: Westernmost island of Japan, closer to Taiwan than to mainland Japan
- **Wildlife**: Hammerhead sharks, turtles, eagle rays

### Izu Peninsula
- **Diving Highlights**: Underwater volcanoes, caves, and tunnels
- **Best Time**: April-November
- **Marine Life**: Tropical and temperate species mix
- **Hot Springs**: Combine diving with onsen (hot spring) experiences

## ☸️ Cultural Experiences

### Temples & Shrines
- **Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)**: Kyoto's most famous temple covered in gold leaf
- **Fushimi Inari Taisha**: Famous for thousands of red torii gates in Kyoto
- **Senso-ji**: Tokyo's oldest temple in Asakusa
- **Kiyomizu-dera**: Wooden temple with panoramic views of Kyoto
- **Toshogu Shrine**: Lavishly decorated shrine in Nikko (UNESCO)

### Traditional Activities
- **Tea Ceremony**: Experience Japanese matcha tea preparation
- **Kimono Rental**: Wear traditional Japanese clothing while exploring
  - **Kyoto**: Rent for ~50€, includes dressing service
  - **Tokyo**: Asakusa area for temple visits
  - **Best Temples**: Kiyomizu-dera, Fushimi Inari, Senso-ji
- **Calligraphy**: Learn Japanese kanji writing
- **Ikebana**: Japanese flower arrangement

### Onsen (Hot Springs)
- **Best Onsen Towns**:
  - **Hakone**: Near Tokyo, views of Mt. Fuji
  - **Beppu**: Kyushu's hot spring capital with "Hell" hot springs
  - **Noboribetsu**: Hokkaido's premier onsen resort
  - **Kusatsu**: Famous for yumomi (water stirring) performances
  - **Yugawara**: Kanagawa prefecture, near Tokyo
  - **Ito**: Shizuoka prefecture, Izu Peninsula
- **Onsen Etiquette**: Wash before entering, no tattoos in many public onsens
- **Tattoo-Friendly**: Ryokan Tokyo (Yugawara), K's House (Ito), Minshuku Miyama (Yamanouchi)

## 🍣 Food & Dining

### Must-Try Dishes

#### Traditional Japanese
- **Sushi**: Fresh raw fish, best at Tsukiji Outer Market (Tokyo) or local sushi bars
- **Ramen**: Noodle soup with various broths (shoyu, miso, tonkotsu, shio)
- **Tempura**: Deep-fried seafood and vegetables
- **Yakitori**: Grilled chicken skewers
- **Okonomiyaki**: Savory pancake with various ingredients
- **Takoyaki**: Octopus-filled dough balls from Osaka

#### Regional Specialties
- **Hokkaido**: Soup curry, Genghis Khan (grilled lamb), fresh seafood
- **Osaka**: Takoyaki, Okonomiyaki, Kushikatsu (deep-fried skewers)
- **Kyoto**: Kaiseki (multi-course meal), Yudofu (tofu hot pot), Matcha sweets
- **Fukuoka**: Hakata ramen (tonkotsu), Mentaiko (spicy cod roe)
- **Okinawa**: Goya champuru (stir-fry), Rafute (braised pork), Awamori (liquor)

#### Unique Experiences
- **Kaiten Sushi**: Conveyor belt sushi, budget-friendly (150¥ for 2 nigiri at Hamazushi)
- **Robot Restaurants**: Themed dining experiences in Tokyo
- **Izakaya**: Japanese-style pub with small plates and drinks
- **Kaiseki**: Traditional multi-course fine dining

### Food Tips
- **7-Eleven/FamilyMart**: Surprisingly good quality convenience store food
- **Vending Machines**: Ramune (Japanese soda), hot coffee, and even ramen
- **Department Store Basements**: Gourmet food halls (depachika) with beautiful presentations

## 🏯 Top Attractions by City

### Tokyo
- **Shibuya Crossing**: World's busiest pedestrian crossing
- **Senso-ji Temple**: Tokyo's oldest temple in Asakusa
- **Meiji Shrine**: Shinto shrine in Shibuya
- **Tokyo Skytree**: Tallest tower in Japan (634m)
- **Tokyo Tower**: Iconic orange and white tower
- **Shinjuku Gyoen**: Beautiful park with various gardens
- **Tsukiji Outer Market**: World's largest fish market (inner market relocated)
- **Akihabara**: Electric town for electronics and anime
- **Odaiba**: Futuristic entertainment and shopping district
- **TeamLab Borderless**: Immersive digital art museum (book early!)

### Kyoto
- **Kinkaku-ji**: Golden Pavilion temple
- **Fushimi Inari**: Thousands of red torii gates
- **Kiyomizu-dera**: Wooden temple with panoramic views
- **Arashiyama Bamboo Forest**: Serene bamboo grove
- **Gion District**: Kyoto's famous geisha district
- **Nijo Castle**: UNESCO World Heritage site with nightingale floors
- **Philosopher's Path**: Scenic canal-side path

### Osaka
- **Osaka Castle**: Historic castle with museum
- **Dotonbori**: Neon lights and street food
- **Universal Studios Japan**: Popular theme park with Japanese and international attractions
- **Shinsekai**: Retro district with Tsutenkaku Tower
- **Umeda Sky Building**: Floating garden observatory
- **Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan**: One of the world's largest aquariums

### Hiroshima
- **Atomic Bomb Dome**: UNESCO World Heritage site, peace memorial
- **Peace Memorial Museum**: Museum about the atomic bombing
- **Miyajima Island**: Famous for the floating torii gate of Itsukushima Shrine
- **Okonomiyaki**: Hiroshima-style layered savory pancake

### Nara
- **Todai-ji Temple**: Houses the world's largest bronze Buddha statue
- **Nara Park**: Friendly bowing deer that roam freely
- **Kasuga Taisha**: Shrine with hundreds of stone and bronze lanterns

### Nikko
- **Toshogu Shrine**: Lavishly decorated UNESCO World Heritage shrine
- **Kegon Falls**: Beautiful 97m waterfall
- **Lake Chuzenji**: Scenic mountain lake
- **Rinno-ji Temple**: Important Buddhist temple

## 🗻 Mount Fuji

### Best Viewpoints
- **Kawaguchiko (Five Lakes Region)**: Best area for Fuji views
  - **Chureito Pagoda**: Iconic red pagoda with Mt. Fuji backdrop (most famous photo spot)
  - **Lake Kawaguchi**: Reflections of Mt. Fuji on the lake surface
  - **Oishi Park**: Seasonal flowers with Fuji views
- **Hakone**: Onsen town with Lake Ashi boat rides offering Fuji views
- **Tokyo**: On clear days, visible from Shinjuku, Shibuya, or Tokyo Skytree

### Best Time for Views
- **Morning**: Best visibility, less clouds
- **Winter (Dec-Feb)**: Clear skies, snow-capped mountain
- **Spring (Apr)**: Cherry blossoms with Mt. Fuji
- **Autumn (Oct)**: Fall colors with Mt. Fuji

### Climbing Mount Fuji
- **Official Season**: July 10 - September 10
- **Trails**: Yoshida (most popular), Subashiri, Gotemba, Fujinomiya
- **Duration**: 5-7 hours up, 3-4 hours down
- **Difficulty**: Moderate to challenging (3,776m elevation)
- **Tips**: Start early, bring warm layers, headlamp for early morning hikes

## 🚆 Transportation

### Japan Rail Pass
- **Cost**: ~50,000¥ for 7 days (ordinary), ~80,000¥ for 14 days
- **Validity**: Only for tourists with temporary visitor visa
- **Coverage**: Most JR trains, including Shinkansen (bullet trains)
- **Purchase**: Must buy before arriving in Japan
- **Activation**: Can activate on any day within 30 days of purchase

### Train Types
- **Shinkansen**: Bullet train (Nozomi, Hikari, Kodama)
- **Limited Express**: Faster than local trains, reserved seating
- **Express**: Faster than local, may require reservation
- **Local/Regional**: Slower, no reservation needed
- **Subway**: In Tokyo, Osaka, and other major cities

### City Transportation
- **Tokyo**: JR Yamanote Line, Tokyo Metro, Toei Subway
- **Osaka**: Osaka Metro, JR Osaka Loop Line
- **Kyoto**: Kyoto City Bus, Karasuma Subway Line
- **IC Cards**: Suica (Tokyo), Pasmo (Tokyo), ICOCA (Osaka) - rechargeable cards for trains/buses

### Domestic Travel
- **Air**: ANA, JAL, Peach Aviation, Vanilla Air
- **Highway Buses**: Overnight buses between major cities
- **Ferries**: To Okinawa, Hokkaido, and remote islands

## 🏨 Accommodation

### Types
- **Ryokan**: Traditional Japanese inn with tatami floors and onsen (hot spring bath)
- **Hotel**: Western-style hotels, from budget to luxury
- **Business Hotel**: Small but efficient rooms, often with free breakfast
- **Capsule Hotel**: Compact sleeping pods, often with shared facilities
- **Minshuku**: Family-run guesthouses, similar to B&Bs
- **Hostel**: Budget accommodation, often with dormitory-style rooms

### Unique Experiences
- **Temple Lodging (Shukubo)**: Stay overnight at Buddhist temples (e.g., Koyasan)
- **Onsen Ryokan**: Traditional inn with private or public hot spring baths
- **Love Hotels**: Short-stay hotels, often themed
- **Farm Stay**: Experience rural Japanese life

### Recommended Areas to Stay
- **Tokyo**: Shinjuku (convenient), Shibuya (young), Asakusa (traditional), Ginza (luxury)
- **Kyoto**: Gion (traditional), near Kyoto Station (convenient)
- **Osaka**: Namba/Dotonbori (nightlife), Umeda (business)

## 💰 Estimated Costs (per day)
- **Budget Backpacker**: ¥5,000-8,000 ($35-55 USD) - Hostels, convenience store meals, local transport
- **Mid-Range**: ¥10,000-20,000 ($70-140 USD) - Business hotels, mix of dining options, some attractions
- **Luxury**: ¥30,000+ ($210+ USD) - Ryokan/5-star hotels, fine dining, multiple attractions

**Money-Saving Tips**:
- Use convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson) for affordable meals
- Travel during off-peak seasons (avoid cherry blossom, Golden Week, Obon)
- Use overnight buses for long-distance travel
- Look for lunch specials (teishoku) at restaurants
- Visit free attractions (temples, parks, city views)

## 💡 Travel Tips

1. **Cash is Still King**: While credit cards are accepted in cities, many rural areas and small businesses are cash-only
2. **Bow Slightly**: A small bow when greeting or thanking someone is polite
3. **No Tipping**: Tipping is not expected and can be refused
4. **Remove Shoes**: Take off shoes when entering homes, ryokan, and some restaurants
5. **Quiet on Public Transport**: Keep phone conversations quiet on trains and buses
6. **No Eating While Walking**: It's considered impolite to eat while walking in public
7. **Learn Basic Phrases**: Simple Japanese phrases are appreciated
   - Konnichiwa (Hello)
   - Arigato gozaimasu (Thank you)
   - Sumimasen (Excuse me/Sorry)
   - Eigo ga hanasemasu ka? (Do you speak English?)

## 🗓️ Festivals & Events

### January
- **New Year's Day (Shogatsu)**: Temples and shrines are crowded with visitors
- **Sapporo Snow Festival**: Giant ice sculptures in Hokkaido

### February
- **Setsubun**: Bean-throwing festival to drive away evil spirits
- **Sapporo Snow Festival (continues)**: If you missed January

### March
- **Hinamatsuri**: Doll's Festival, celebrating girls

### April
- **Cherry Blossom Festivals**: Hanami parties across the country

### May
- **Golden Week**: Series of national holidays, busy travel period

### July
- **Gion Matsuri**: Kyoto's famous festival with elaborate floats
- **Tanabata**: Star festival with colorful decorations
- **Fireworks Festivals**: Various locations, especially Sumida River (Tokyo)

### August
- **Obon**: Buddhist festival honoring ancestors, many businesses closed
- **Nebuta Matsuri**: Aomori's spectacular illuminated float parade
- **Tenjin Matsuri**: Osaka's boat procession festival

### September
- **Tsukimi**: Moon-viewing festivals

### October
- **Autumn Festivals**: Celebrating harvest season
- **Jidai Matsuri**: Kyoto's Festival of the Ages

### November
- **Shichi-Go-San**: Festival for children's growth and health

### December
- **Christmas Illuminations**: Beautiful light displays in major cities
- **Omisoka**: New Year's Eve celebrations
