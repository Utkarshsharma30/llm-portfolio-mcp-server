# Delhi – Complete Travel Guide

*Source: [Distant Journeys - Delhi Travel Guide](https://www.distantjourneys.co.uk/india-tours/destination-guides/delhi-travel-guide/)*  
*Added: August 30, 2026*  
*Region: Northern India | Status: National Capital Territory*

## 📍 Overview

**Delhi, India's vibrant National Capital Territory**, is a city steeped in history and energy. With over 20 million residents, it's a bustling metropolis that blends ancient monuments with dynamic modern life.

From the minarets of Jama Masjid to the street food of Chandni Chowk, there are countless things to do in Delhi. Whether you're diving into local cuisine, exploring centuries-old forts, or taking in its diverse neighbourhoods, Delhi offers an unforgettable experience for every kind of traveller.

Delhi lies in northern India, nestled along the **Yamuna River** and bordered by Haryana and Uttar Pradesh. It's both the capital of India and a cultural epicentre, attracting visitors worldwide with its deep-rooted heritage.

Thanks to its central location, Delhi is a springboard for exploring other must-visit destinations. We recommend adding **Agra** (home of the Taj Mahal, 3-4 hours from Delhi) and **Jaipur** (known for its palaces and forts, part of the Golden Triangle) to your itinerary.

Delhi is the starting point for many tours, including the Golden Triangle, Temples & Tigers, and Grand Tour of India.

## 🌤️ Best Time to Visit Delhi

Delhi's climate ranges from intense summer heat to cool winters.

### Summer (April–June)
- **Temperature**: Often exceeding 40°C
- **Condition**: Extremely hot
- **Recommendation**: Avoid if possible

### Monsoon (July–September)
- **Condition**: Heavy rains
- **Pros**: Welcome relief from summer heat
- **Cons**: Can disrupt travel plans

### Winter (October–March) ⭐ *Best Time*
- **Temperature**: Cool and pleasant
- **Significance**: Peak tourist season
- **Festivals**: Diwali, Holi
- **Note**: The ideal time to visit, when many of Delhi's most celebrated festivals take place, adding colour and culture to your trip

## 🏛️ What is Delhi Known For?

Delhi is renowned for its **UNESCO-listed landmarks**, bustling markets, and culinary heritage. Explore sites like the Red Fort, Qutub Minar, and Humayun's Tomb, or dive into India's story at museums like the National Museum and Gandhi Smriti.

**New Delhi**, the administrative centre of the capital, offers wide boulevards, parliament buildings, and government offices alongside impressive Mughal-era architecture. This contrast between old and new is part of what makes Delhi so captivating.

### UNESCO World Heritage Sites
- **Red Fort**
- **Qutub Minar**
- **Humayun's Tomb**

### Dual Personality
- **Old Delhi**: Historic walled city with Mughal-era architecture
- **New Delhi**: Administrative centre with wide boulevards, parliament buildings

## 🏆 Best Places to Visit in Delhi

From ancient temples and monuments to bustling bazaars and markets, Delhi is a vibrant and exciting city that is sure to delight visitors of all ages.

### 1. 🏰 Red Fort
![Red Fort, Delhi](https://cdn.distantjourneys.co.uk/7583f08a-cfc5-44e8-87d2-b22100e20d9f/iStock-623585986_Original%20file.jpg)

The Red Fort is a beautiful and imposing **red sandstone fortress** located in Old Delhi. Built in the **17th century** by the Mughal emperor **Shah Jahan**, the Red Fort is a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** and one of the most iconic landmarks in Delhi.

- **Status**: UNESCO World Heritage Site
- **Built**: 17th century
- **Builder**: Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan
- **Open**: Every day except Monday
- **Highlights**:
  - Fascinating museums and galleries inside
  - Beautiful gardens including Hayat Bakhsh Bagh and Moti Masjid
  - Historical significance as Mughal imperial residence

### 2. 🕌 Jama Masjid
One of **India's largest mosques**, Jama Masjid can host **25,000 worshippers**. Its grand courtyard and towering minarets offer a perfect introduction to Mughal design.

- **Significance**: One of India's largest mosques
- **Capacity**: 25,000 worshippers
- **Architecture**: Grand courtyard, towering minarets
- **Dress Code**: Modest clothing required
- **Requirement**: Remove shoes before entering
- **Experience**: Perfect introduction to Mughal architectural design

### 3. 🛍️ Chandni Chowk
As one of the **oldest and busiest markets** in Delhi, it's easy to see why Chandni Chowk is a popular tourist destination. The market is known for its lively atmosphere. Expect narrow streets and alleyways packed with vendors, selling everything from spices and sweets to clothing and electronics.

- **Significance**: One of the oldest and busiest markets in Delhi
- **Atmosphere**: Lively, bustling, authentic
- **What to Find**: Spices, sweets, clothing, electronics
- **Food Scene**: Wide variety of restaurants and street food vendors offering delicious local cuisine
- **Note**: Some of the best street food in India can be found here

### 4. 🏛️ India Gate & Parliament House
Often dubbed as two of the most important and popular tourist attractions in Delhi, **India Gate** and **Parliament House** offer a fascinating glimpse into the history of this impressive country.

- **India Gate**: War memorial and national symbol of sacrifice and patriotism
  - **Height**: 42 metres
  - **Built**: Early 20th century
  - **Purpose**: Commemorates Indian soldiers
  
- **Parliament House**: Current seat of the Indian government
  - **Architecture**: Circular shape, central dome
  - **Surroundings**: Well-manicured gardens

Both the India Gate and Parliament House are must-see attractions for anyone visiting Delhi.

### 5. 🏛️ Qutub Minar
![Qutub Minar, Delhi](https://cdn.distantjourneys.co.uk/5da0b48e-f46f-451e-87cd-b22100e21dc8/iStock-680778472_Original%20file.jpg)

Built in the **13th century**, Qutub Minar is a towering minaret in the heart of Delhi. It's one of the most iconic landmarks in the city, recognised by its **UNESCO World Heritage status**, making it a must-see spot in Delhi!

- **Status**: UNESCO World Heritage Site
- **Built**: 13th century
- **Height**: 72.5 metres tall
- **Material**: Impressive red sandstone and marble
- **Complex**: Surrounded by ancient ruins
- **Nearby Attractions**:
  - Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque
  - Iron Pillar of Delhi

### 6. 🏛️ Humayun's Tomb
Humayun's Tomb is a beautiful **Mughal-style tomb** located in the heart of Delhi. Built in the **16th century**, the tomb is another impressive **UNESCO World Heritage Site** in the city.

- **Built**: 16th century
- **Status**: UNESCO World Heritage Site
- **Architecture**: Striking Mughal-style architecture
- **Features**: Intricate carvings
- **Significance**: Final resting place of Mughal emperor Humayun

### 7. 🪷 Lotus Temple
![Lotus Temple, Delhi](https://cdn.distantjourneys.co.uk/8035471e-37e1-4cea-a15f-b22000eb0f10/AdobeStock_186039608_Original%20file.jpeg)

This modern **Baha'i House of Worship** is admired for its distinctive **lotus-shaped design**, which symbolises the unity of all nations. The temple is open to people of all faiths and offers a peaceful and contemplative space for prayer and meditation.

- **Type**: Modern Baha'i House of Worship
- **Design**: Distinctive lotus-shaped architecture
- **Symbolism**: Unity of all nations
- **Accessibility**: Open to people of all faiths
- **Experience**: Peaceful and contemplative space

![Rashtrapati Bhavan, New Delhi](https://cdn.distantjourneys.co.uk/4d283b69-e754-4842-93ab-b22000e62c7d/AdobeStock_135056414_Original%20file.jpeg)

## 🍽️ Food & Drink in Delhi

![Delhi Food](https://cdn.distantjourneys.co.uk/d1466e8e-cb8b-4156-9256-b222008941c9/AdobeStock_133451950_Original%20file.jpeg)

Delhi is a **food-lover's paradise**, with a rich culinary heritage rooted in North Indian traditions. The city's bold, flavour-packed dishes span everything from spicy street snacks to rich Mughal-inspired curries.

You'll find countless places to enjoy **chaats, kebabs, biryanis, and parathas** – all bursting with spice and character. Don't miss the opportunity to sip on a roadside **chai**, typically made with black tea, cardamom, ginger, and milk.

For dessert, try **jalebi, gulab jamun** or **kulfi** – all local favourites. Whether you choose high-end dining or quick bites from market stalls, Delhi's food scene is as unforgettable as its history.

### Must-Try Dishes
- **Chaats**: Savory street food snacks (golgappe, bhel puri, papdi chat)
- **Kebabs**: Grilled meat skewers (seekh kebab, shami kebab)
- **Biryanis**: Fragrant rice dishes with meat or vegetables
- **Parathas**: Stuffed flatbreads (aloo, gobhi, paneer)
- **Chai**: Spiced tea with cardamom, ginger, and milk
- **Jalebi**: Deep-fried sweet syrup dessert
- **Gulab Jamun**: Sweet syrup-soaked dumplings
- **Kulfi**: Traditional Indian ice cream

### Recommended Food Experiences
- **Street Food Tour**: Explore Chandni Chowk's food stalls
- **Fine Dining**: Indian Accent, Bukhara, Karim's
- **Cafe Culture**: India Coffee House, Indian Habitat Centre
- **Rooftop Dining**: Views of Delhi skyline with dinner

## 🚗 Getting Around Delhi

### Public Transport
- **Metro**: Most efficient way to travel, extensive network
- **Buses**: DTC and cluster buses, budget-friendly
- **Auto-rickshaws**: Three-wheeled taxis, negotiate fares
- **Taxis**: Prepaid taxis available at airports and major hotels

### Private Transport
- **Car Hire**: Self-drive or with driver
- **Ride-hailing**: Uber, Ola widely available
- **Private Tours**: Guided tours with transportation included

### Walking
- **Old Delhi**: Walkable but crowded, best with guide
- **New Delhi**: Wide boulevards, pedestrian-friendly areas
- **Safety**: Generally safe during daylight hours

## 🎒 Travel Tips for Delhi

### Essential Tips
1. **Plan your itinerary** - Delhi is huge, prioritize your must-see attractions
2. **Dress appropriately** - Modest clothing recommended for religious sites
3. **Stay hydrated** - Carry water bottle, especially in summer
4. **Use reputable transport** - Prepaid taxis, metro, or ride-hailing apps
5. **Bargain wisely** - Bargaining is expected in markets
6. **Carry identification** - Police may ask for ID at security checkpoints

### Health & Safety
- **Food**: Eat at busy restaurants, avoid raw salads from street vendors
- **Water**: Drink bottled or filtered water
- **Air Quality**: Check pollution levels, use masks if needed
- **Scams**: Beware of touts, use official guides

### Best Practices
- **Temples**: Remove shoes, cover shoulders and knees
- **Photography**: Ask permission before photographing people
- **Tipping**: 10% in restaurants, small amounts for drivers/guides
- **Negotiation**: Politely bargain in markets and with auto-rickshaws
