# Delhi

**Delhi** is the capital city of India and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. With a history spanning over 5,000 years, Delhi offers an overwhelming amount of history, culture, life, food, temples, and everything else you could possibly imagine.

*Source: [25 Incredible Things To Do In Delhi, India](https://migrationology.com/25-things-to-do-in-delhi-india/) by Mark Wiens*

## Overview

Delhi is a giant metropolis that serves as the political, cultural, and commercial center of India. The city is a fascinating blend of ancient history and modern development, with Mughal-era monuments standing alongside contemporary buildings. While visiting Delhi can present challenges - things don't always turn out as planned and frustrations can arise - the culture, history, and food make it an incredible destination.

**Population**: ~30 million (National Capital Region)
**Best Time to Visit**: October to March (cooler temperatures)
**Language**: Hindi, English widely spoken

## Transportation

### Metro
The Delhi Metro is clean, efficient, often packed, and extremely cheap. To make travel easier:
- Buy a Metro card (50 Rupees deposit) and add cash
- 1-day tourist passes: 100 Rupees
- 3-day tourist passes: 250 Rupees

After getting off the Metro, you can either walk or take a short auto rickshaw ride to most attractions.

### HOHO Bus (Hop On Hop Off)
- Tourist bus with 1-day or 2-day passes
- Goes from attraction to attraction
- Buses come every 30-45 minutes
- **Note**: Does not run on Mondays

### Auto Rickshaws
- Always negotiate the price before getting in
- Short rides typically cost 30-50 INR

## Top 25 Things to Do in Delhi

### Temples & Religious Sites

#### 1. The Lotus Temple (Bahá'í House of Worship)
- **Description**: Designed in the shape of a sacred lotus flower with 27 marble petals
- **Religion**: Open to all religions (Bahá'í philosophy)
- **Features**: Nicely manicured gardens, free shoe deposit, silent sanctuary
- **How to get there**: Metro to Kalkaji Mandir station, 10-minute walk
- **Website**: [bahaihouseofworship.in](http://www.bahaihouseofworship.in/)
- **Hours**: Tuesday-Sunday 9am-7pm, closed Mondays
- **Price**: Free

#### 2. ISKCON Hare Krishna Temple
- **Description**: One of the largest temples dedicated to the International Society for Krishna Consciousness
- **Features**: Free to walk around, sit in sanctuary, listen to drum and chant of "Har-e-Krishna"
- **How to get there**: Closest Metro station is Nehru Place, 10-minute walk
- **Website**: [iskcondelhi.com](http://www.iskcondelhi.com/)
- **Address**: Hare Krishna Hill Sant Nagar, East of Kailash New Delhi
- **Hours**: Day hours
- **Price**: Free

#### 3. Kalkaji Mandir Hindu Temple
- **Description**: Dedicated to the Goddess Kali, mostly visited by locals
- **Features**: Site of many rituals including singing and chanting
- **How to get there**: Metro to Kalkaji Mandir station, 5-minute walk (across from Lotus Temple entrance)
- **Hours**: Tuesday-Sunday morning to night, closed Mondays
- **Price**: Free

#### 4. Jama Masjid
- **Description**: Largest Islamic mosque in Delhi, built in the 17th century
- **Features**: Huge structure with towering minarets and onion-shaped domes, capacity for 25,000 people
- **Note**: Camera fee is 300 INR, but entrance is free if not taking photos
- **How to get there**: Metro to Chawri Chowk, exit Gate #3, walk 10-15 minutes
- **Hours**: 8am-30 minutes before sunset
- **Price**: Free to enter, 300 INR for photos

#### 5. Akshardham Temple
- **Description**: Modern Hindu temple complex built in 2005 with elaborate architecture
- **Features**: Secure free storage for belongings, stunning interior with unimaginable detail
- **Note**: No photos allowed inside
- **How to get there**: Metro to Akshardham station, 10-minute walk
- **Website**: [akshardham.com](http://www.akshardham.com/)
- **Hours**: 9:30am-6:30pm, closed Monday
- **Price**: Free

#### 6. Gurudwara Bangla Sahib (Sikh Temple)
- **Description**: One of the most significant Sikh temples in Delhi
- **Features**: Friendly, inviting atmosphere, huge pool of water, dining facility, medical facility
- **How to get there**: Metro to Patel Chowk, 10-15 minute walk along Ashok Road
- **Hours**: Daytime hours
- **Price**: Free

#### 7. Laxmi Narayan Mandir (Birla Mandir)
- **Description**: Significant Hindu temple complex, officially opened by Mahatma Gandhi
- **Features**: Hindu temple for people of all castes
- **Note**: Shoe storage expects a tip
- **How to get there**: Auto rickshaw from Sikh Temple for 40 INR
- **Hours**: 6am-10pm daily
- **Price**: Free (tip for shoes expected)

### Historical Sites

#### 8. Hauz Khas Complex
- **Description**: Ancient community of ruins including a mosque, various tombs, and a historic water tank
- **History**: Built in early 1300s to store rainwater
- **Features**: Now a public park, popular with students
- **How to get there**: Metro to Green Park, then auto rickshaw (20-30 INR)
- **Website**: [hauzkhasvillage.in](http://hauzkhasvillage.in/)
- **Hours**: Daylight hours
- **Price**: Free

#### 9. Lodi Gardens
- **Description**: Public park scattered with ancient Mughal tombs and structures
- **Features**: Kids play, picnics, artists find inspiration, large park for walking
- **How to get there**: Metro to Khan Market, 30-minute walk or auto rickshaw
- **Hours**: Daylight hours
- **Price**: Free

#### 10. India Gate
- **Description**: 42-meter tall war memorial built in 1931
- **Features**: Honors Indian soldiers, continual flame under the gate
- **Warning**: Many beggars and pushy sellers in the area
- **How to get there**: Nearest Metro is Central Secretariat, 30-minute walk along Rajpath
- **Hours**: Daylight hours
- **Price**: Free

#### 11. Jantar Mantar
- **Description**: Complex of structures used for astronomy observations
- **History**: Commissioned by Mughal emperor Muhammad Shah, constructed by Maharaja Jai Singh II
- **Features**: 13 different instruments for sun, moon, and planet measurements
- **How to get there**: Metro to Rajiv Chowk or Patel Chowk, short walk along Sansad Marg
- **Address**: Sansad Marg, Connaught Place, New Delhi
- **Website**: [jantarmantar.org](http://www.jantarmantar.org/)
- **Hours**: Daylight hours
- **Price**: 100 INR

#### 12. Qutub Minar
- **Description**: 72.5-meter minaret, tallest in India, built nearly 1,000 years ago
- **Features**: Constructed from red sandstone with detailed carvings, UNESCO World Heritage Site
- **Note**: Cannot go inside, but impressive from distance and up close
- **How to get there**: Metro to Qutub Minar station, then auto rickshaw (30-40 INR)
- **Address**: Mehrauli, New Delhi, Delhi 110030
- **Hours**: 10am-6pm daily
- **Price**: 250 INR (count your change!)

#### 13. Humayun's Tomb
- **Description**: Persian-designed tomb built in mid-1500s for Mughal emperor Humayun
- **Features**: Surrounded by gardens, climb stairs to main deck, see marble tombstones inside
- **How to get there**: No direct Metro, closest is Khan Market or JLN Stadium, then auto rickshaw
- **Address**: Mathura Road, Nizamuddin, New Delhi
- **Hours**: 6am-6pm daily
- **Price**: 250 INR (UNESCO World Heritage Site)

#### 14. Tomb of Safdarjung
- **Description**: Built in 1754 for Safdarjung, a prominent leader under Muhammad Shah
- **Features**: Similar construction to Humayun's tomb, surrounded by Mughal gardens
- **How to get there**: Metro to Safdarjung station, exit Gate 2, u-turn, few hundred meters walk
- **Hours**: Daylight hours
- **Price**: 100 INR

#### 15. Red Fort (Lal Qila)
- **Description**: Red sandstone Mughal fortress with walls surrounding an ancient city
- **Features**: Walk around, view main structures, halls, mosques, quiet escape from crowds
- **How to get there**: Walk 20 minutes or auto rickshaw from Chandni Chowk Metro to Lahore Gate
- **Address**: Netaji Subhash Rd, Chandni Chowk, New Delhi, Delhi 110006
- **Hours**: Tuesday-Sunday daylight hours, closed Mondays
- **Price**: 250 INR

### Museums & Cultural Sites

#### 16. National Museum of New Delhi
- **Description**: Impressive collection of over 200,000 individual pieces of art
- **Note**: The author didn't visit, but recommends for art and history lovers
- **How to get there**: Metro to Central Secretariat, walk towards Rajpath to Janpath
- **Address**: Janpath Road, Rajghat, New Delhi 110002
- **Website**: [nationalmuseumindia.gov.in](http://www.nationalmuseumindia.gov.in/)
- **Hours**: 10am-5pm Tuesday-Sunday, closed Monday
- **Price**: 300 INR (includes audio tour)

#### 17. Raj Ghat - Gandhi Memorial
- **Description**: Memorial dedicated to Mahatma Gandhi at the place where he was cremated
- **Features**: Park area, leave shoes at counter for small tip, walk around black marble memorial
- **How to get there**: Auto rickshaw from Chandni Chowk (60 INR), or to Indraprasta leaving (40 INR)
- **Hours**: 5:30am-7pm daily
- **Price**: Free (20 INR tip for shoe man)

### Markets & Shopping

#### 18. Chandni Chowk & Chawri Bazar
- **Description**: Dense chaotic center of trade, business, food, spices, and life
- **Features**: Narrow ancient lanes, interesting people and businesses, visit Jama Masjid and Red Fort nearby
- **Tip**: Don't miss Paranthe Wali Gali or chaat from Natraj
- **How to get there**: Metro to Chawri Bazar or Chandni Chowk station
- **Hours**: Day and night, recommended late morning to afternoon

#### 19. Spice Market (Khari Baoli in Old Delhi)
- **Description**: Largest spice market in Asia
- **Features**: Colorful array of spices, dried fruits, nuts, rickshaw transporters
- **How to get there**: Metro to Chandni Chowk, walk west along Chandni Chowk road to T-intersection, right on Mission Church Road, left on Khari Baoli

#### 20. Dilli Haat - Handicraft Village
- **Description**: Outdoor shopping plaza with high-quality Indian handicrafts
- **Features**: Safe, organized, convenient environment, represents each state of India
- **Food**: Sample Indian food from around the entire nation
- **How to get there**: Metro to INA station, short walk
- **Address**: Sri Aurobindo Marg, West Kidwai Nagar, New Delhi
- **Website**: [dillihaat.net.in](http://www.dillihaat.net.in/)
- **Hours**: 10:30am-10pm daily
- **Price**: 20 INR entrance fee

### Modern Areas

#### 21. Connaught Place - Rajiv Chowk (& Palika Bazar)
- **Description**: Central business district with series of roundabouts
- **Features**: Countless stores, delicious restaurants, dark underground shopping plaza (Palika Bazar)
- **Note**: Can be confusing for first-time visitors
- **How to get there**: Metro to Rajiv Chowk station, any exit
- **Hours**: Shops and restaurants start opening at 10-11am daily

#### 22. Paharganj (Main Bazar)
- **Description**: District next to New Delhi Railroad Station, budget backpacker center
- **Features**: Crowded streets, shopping, many businesses and restaurants
- **Food Tip**: Best chole bhature at Sita Ram Diwan Chand
- **How to get there**: Metro to New Delhi Station, walk through railroad station to other side, cross street
- **Road**: Main Bazar or Baba Namdev Marg

### Food Experiences

#### 23. Kulle Chaat
- **Description**: Various vegetables and fruits hollowed out and filled with blend of spices, lime juice, salt, chickpeas, and pomegranate seeds
- **Note**: Simple but life-changingly delicious
- **Warning**: May contain unclean water, be careful if you have a sensitive stomach
- **How to get there**: Metro to Chawri Chowk, Gate 3, walk straight down hectic road on left side
- **Price**: 50 INR

#### 24. Delhi Street Food
- **Description**: Marvelous array of Indian street food
- **Popular Items**: Chole (spicy chickpea curry), samosas, pakoras, various chaats
- **Best Location**: Chandni Chowk for diverse selection
- **Prices**: 30-50 INR for snacks, 40-60 INR for meals
- **Note**: Delhi street food is slightly more expensive than Kolkata

#### 25. North Indian Food
- **Description**: Rich, flavorful, and very satisfying cuisine
- **Features**: Breads like tandoori roti, chapatis, naan, fried breads more common than rice
- **Dishes**: Tandoori cooked chicken, paneer cheese in various curries, legumes like lentils, beans, and peas
- **Recommendations**: Karim's in Old Delhi, Bhape Da Hotel at Connaught Place
- **Note**: Both pure vegetarian and non-vegetarian restaurants available

## Practical Tips

1. **Count Your Change**: Officials may try to shortchange tourists. Always count your money.
2. **Dress Modestly**: Especially for women, avoid fancy or revealing clothing. Consider a head covering/scarf.
3. **Stay Positive**: Things won't always go as planned, but keep a positive attitude.
4. **Bargain**: Haggling is expected and can be fun. Typically pay about 1/3 of the starting price.
5. **Safety**: Generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas.
