# South Asia

**South Asia**, also known as the **Indian subcontinent** (or "the subcontinent"), is a triangular landmass bordered by the Himalayas in the north, the Indian Ocean in the south, and the Ganges and Indus river valleys in east and west.

*Source: [South Asia - Travel guide at Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/South_Asia)*

## Overview

Home to 2.08 billion people (approximately 26% of the world population), South Asia has the world's tallest mountains and is the origin of some of the world's oldest civilizations. The region is the wellspring of major world religions including Hinduism, Buddhism, Sanamahism, Sikhism, and Jainism.

## History

South Asia is the origin of four major religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism, and Jainism. Islam was introduced by invading Arabs starting around the 7th century and rose to prominence during the Mughal Empire.

The region has a complex colonial history:

- **British Rule**: Much of South Asia was under British rule, with even nominally independent areas under British suzerainty
- **Jewel in the Crown**: India was considered the "Jewel in the Crown" of the British Empire
- **Partition**: In 1947, the region was partitioned into Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan, leading to massive migration and bloodshed
- **Further Divisions**: The 1971 war resulted in East Pakistan becoming Bangladesh
- **Independence**: Sri Lanka (Ceylon) became independent in 1948, Maldives in 1965

### Nepal and Bhutan
- Never formally colonized by the British
- Were under British suzerainty during the colonial era
- Nepal provided Gurkha soldiers to the British Army
- Bhutan's foreign policy was controlled by the British, then passed to India after independence

## Climate

Apart from the Himalayas, the climate is tropical with:
- **Monsoon in summer** and **dry winter**
- **Western Pakistan**: Monsoon is quite non-existent
- **Southern India**: Monsoon lasts for six months
- **Sri Lanka**: Has two monsoons - one in May, one in October/November

The region experiences diverse weather patterns due to its varied geography.

## People

South Asia is one of the world's most densely populated regions:
- **Population**: About 2.08 billion people (roughly a quarter of humanity)
- **Population Density**: 305/km² (790/sq mi) - 7 times the world average
- **Urban Features**: Very common to find narrow alleys with buildings wrapped in electric wires

## Languages

The region does not have a single lingua franca, but English is widely spoken by educated people due to British colonial influence.

- India alone is home to hundreds of languages

- In major cities and tourist destinations, you can generally get by with English
- It's rare for locals to speak Western European languages other than English
- Some elderly in Pondicherry (former French India) still know French
- Some elderly in Goa (former Portuguese colony) may speak Portuguese

## Getting In

### Overland

Getting in overland to South Asia can be challenging:

- **From the West**: Relatively easy, especially from Istanbul, Turkey to New Delhi, India (also known as the Hippie Trail)
- **From Northwest**: Borders Afghanistan, which has been a war zone for several decades
- **From China**: Possible via Karakoram Highway to Nepal, but requires special permit to travel through Tibet
- **From Myanmar**: Quite a lot of bureaucracy reportedly required

## Getting Around

### By Train

Rail transport varies by country:
- **India**: Most extensive rail network in South Asia (see Rail travel in India)
- **Pakistan**: Rail network mostly underdeveloped
- **Sri Lanka**: Efficient and cheap train network with beautiful hill country and tea plantation scenery
- **Bangladesh**: Famous for its overcrowded trains
- **Maldives, Nepal, Bhutan**: No trains at all

## Nature

South Asia offers dramatic natural beauty:
- **World's tallest mountains** (Himalayas, Karakoram)
- **Coastal regions** with the world's highest rainfall
- Part of the **Indomalayan wildlife region**

**Iconic Species**:
- Asian elephants
- Tigers
- Peacocks

**Notable for**:
- **Nepal** and **Kashmir**: World renowned for mountaineering

## Religion and Spirituality

South Asia is the birthplace of major religious movements:
- **Hinduism**
- **Buddhism**
- **Sikhism**
- **Jainism**

These have a long history and intercontinental appeal since the 20th century. Many visitors come to South Asia to learn **yoga** - a group of physical, mental and spiritual practices from ancient India.

## Sports

Popular sports in South Asia:

- **Cricket**: Huge business in South Asia, with India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka boasting world-class national teams

## Eat

You will not be able to experience the best of South Asian culture until you have consumed its flavorful food. India alone has some of the best cuisines in the world, with incredible diversity throughout the region.

**Notable Cuisines**:
- Indian: North and South have distinct styles
- Pakistani: Similar to North Indian
- Bangladeshi: Rich and flavorful
- Sri Lankan: Spicy and diverse
- Nepali: Influenced by Indian and Tibetan
- Bhutanese: Unique and spicy

## Drink

- **Tea** (chai): Common beverage consumed throughout the region
- **Alcohol**: Banned or restricted in Muslim communities and some Hindu-majority areas (e.g., Gujarat)
- **Fruit juices**: Ubiquitous throughout the region

## Stay Safe

**Generally Safe Areas**: Most of the region is considered safe for visitors

**Areas to Avoid**:
- **Northwest Pakistan**: Close to the border with Afghanistan, war-zone controlled by extremist militant groups such as Tehreek-e-Taliban
- **Parts of Kashmir**: Can be unsafe due to ongoing conflicts

**General Advice**:
- Avoid political demonstrations
- Be cautious in border areas
- Check travel advisories before visiting
- Respect local customs and dress codes
